How to Prepare Your Phone for International Travel?

✓ Practical guidance⌖ International travel✦ Updated August 2026

Your phone can become one ↗ of your most important travel tools abroad. It may hold your boarding pass, hotel address, maps, translation tools, payment apps, transport tickets, photos, and the phone number you use for account verification. Preparing it before departure can prevent small technical problems from becoming much bigger problems after you land.

When I prepare a phone for an international trip, I focus on three things first: can I get online, can I still access important information without internet, and can I recover the phone or my accounts if something goes wrong? I would rather solve those questions at home on reliable Wi-Fi than stand in an airport trying to remember a password or work out why mobile data is not connecting.

For this guide, I checked current iPhone and Android guidance available in August 2026 and organized the preparation around practical travel situations. You do not need to change every setting on your phone. The goal is to make the phone more reliable abroad without turning preparation into another complicated project.

!

Phone Preparation Note: Settings names and available features can differ by phone model, operating system, carrier, and country. Check the exact options on the device you are actually taking.

Key Takeaways

  • Update your phone and important apps before departure, not at the airport.

  • Make a fresh backup before traveling.

  • Free enough storage for photos, videos, offline maps, language downloads, and app updates.

  • Use a strong screen lock and biometrics where supported.

  • Turn on Apple's Find My or Google's Find Hub before the phone goes missing.

  • Make sure you know how to recover your Apple or Google account without relying only on the phone you are carrying.

  • Decide how you will get mobile data before departure.

  • Check whether your phone is carrier unlocked if you plan to use another carrier.

  • Review roaming and Dual SIM settings before leaving your home network.

  • Keep important travel information available offline.

  • Do not depend on one phone for the only copy of every booking and document.

  • Test your setup at home while you still have reliable Wi-Fi and another device available.

What Does “Preparing Your Phone for International Travel” Actually Mean?

It does not mean installing twenty travel apps.

A properly prepared phone should be ready for a few common situations.

After Landing

Can you:

  • Connect to mobile data?

  • Find your hotel?

  • Contact someone?

  • Open your booking?

Without Internet

Can you still access:

  • Accommodation address?

  • Important reservation details?

  • Useful maps?

  • Essential travel information?

If the Phone Is Lost

Can you:

  • Locate it?

  • Lock it?

  • Access your main account from another device?

  • Contact your mobile provider?

  • Continue the trip without losing every important detail?

That is the standard I would use.

Start at Least a Few Days Before Departure

I would not leave phone preparation until the night before the flight.

Some steps can require time.

For example:

  • A large backup may take hours.

  • An operating-system update may need several gigabytes.

  • Your carrier may need time to unlock the phone.

  • An eSIM installation may need troubleshooting.

  • You may discover your storage is almost full.

  • An important password may need resetting.

Starting a few days early gives you time to fix problems without pressure.

1. Update Your Phone Before the Trip

I prefer traveling with a reasonably current operating system.

Updates can include:

  • Security fixes

  • Network improvements

  • Bug fixes

  • Compatibility updates

Travel is not the ideal time to discover that:

  • A transport app no longer supports your software.

  • Your eSIM setup has a known bug.

  • A payment app requires a newer version.

Update before departure while you have:

  • Reliable Wi-Fi

  • A charger

  • Time to solve unexpected problems

Do Not Start a Major Update Right Before Leaving for the Airport

Updating is useful.

Updating thirty minutes before you need the phone is not.

A large update can:

  • Take longer than expected.

  • Restart the phone.

  • Require additional setup.

  • Temporarily use significant battery.

I would complete it earlier and then use the phone normally for a day or two.

That gives you a chance to notice any problem.

Update Important Travel Apps Too

Open your app store and update the apps you expect to rely on.

That may include:

  • Airline app

  • Hotel or booking app

  • Navigation app

  • Translation app

  • Messaging app

  • Banking or payment app

  • Transport app

  • Password manager

I do not need every game and entertainment app updated before traveling.

Prioritize the tools that could affect the trip.

2. Free Up Phone Storage

Running out of storage abroad is surprisingly inconvenient.

Travel can create large amounts of new data through:

  • Photos

  • Videos

  • Offline maps

  • Downloaded language packs

  • Boarding documents

  • Music and movies

If the phone is nearly full before departure, the problem will only become worse during the trip.

Check Storage Before You Leave

Look at your phone's storage settings.

Pay attention to large categories such as:

  • Photos and videos

  • Downloaded media

  • Old apps

  • Messaging attachments

  • Offline files

You do not need to make the phone empty.

You simply want enough free space that ordinary travel use does not constantly trigger storage warnings.

Delete Downloads You No Longer Need

Common examples include:

  • Old movies

  • Podcasts

  • Offline playlists

  • Downloaded documents

  • Old offline maps

These can quietly take several gigabytes.

Remove what is no longer useful before adding travel content.

Be Careful About Deleting Photos Before Checking the Backup

Do not delete thousands of photos just because storage is low without understanding where the originals are stored.

Confirm whether they are:

  • Backed up

  • Synced

  • Stored elsewhere

first.

Free storage deliberately rather than creating another data problem.

3. Make a Fresh Backup Before International Travel

This is one of the highest-value steps in the entire process.

If the phone is:

  • Lost

  • Stolen

  • Damaged

  • Dropped in water

a recent backup can make replacing it much easier.

Apple currently supports backing up an iPhone through iCloud or a computer, while Android devices can back up supported device data to a Google Account.

Do Not Assume Automatic Backup Means the Latest Backup Is Recent

Open the backup settings and check.

Look for:

  • Whether backup is enabled

  • Which account is being used

  • When the last backup happened

If necessary, start a manual backup.

On Android, Google notes that a device backup can take up to 24 hours in some cases, which is another reason not to leave this until the last minute.

Check What Is Actually Included

Not every app stores everything inside the normal phone backup.

Think separately about:

  • Photos

  • Messaging history

  • Authenticator apps

  • Important files

  • Notes

  • Password manager

  • App-specific data

If one of these matters strongly to you, check its own backup or sync system.

Your Photos Deserve Their Own Check

Photos can quickly become the most valuable data created during a trip.

Decide how you will protect them.

Options can include:

  • Cloud photo backup

  • Computer backup

  • Another storage system

If automatic cloud photo backup is enabled, remember that uploading many videos over mobile data can consume a travel data allowance quickly.

You may prefer uploads only on Wi-Fi.

4. Use a Strong Screen Lock

Your phone contains much more than travel photos.

It may provide access to:

  • Email

  • Banking

  • Messages

  • Password resets

  • Cloud storage

  • Travel bookings

  • Payment cards

A simple swipe lock is not enough.

Use:

  • PIN

  • Passcode

  • Password

  • Biometrics

according to what your device supports.

Google specifically recommends a PIN, pattern, or password rather than an unprotected swipe for stronger backup and lost-device protection.

Use Biometrics for Convenience, Not Instead of a Passcode

Fingerprint or facial recognition can make normal travel use faster.

But your underlying passcode still matters.

You may need it after:

  • Restarting the phone

  • Certain security events

  • Changing settings

Make sure it is something you actually remember.

Avoid an Obvious Passcode

I would not use:

  • 1234

  • 0000

  • Birth year

for a phone holding important travel and account information.

The inconvenience of entering a stronger code is small compared with the amount of information the phone protects.

5. Turn On Lost-Phone Tracking Before You Travel

This feature cannot help properly if you only think about it after the phone disappears.

iPhone

Make sure Find My is enabled.

Apple's current Find My system can locate supported devices, play a sound, and help place a missing iPhone into Lost Mode. Find My needs to be set up before the device is lost.

Android

Make sure Find Hub is enabled and that the phone appears in your Google account.

Google's current system can help locate, ring, mark as lost, or erase a compatible Android device. It can also use encrypted recent locations and its broader finding network for supported offline-device scenarios.

Test the Finding Service Before Departure

Do not simply assume it works.

From another device or browser:

  • Sign in.

  • Confirm your phone appears.

  • Check that the account recognizes it.

You do not need to remotely erase anything.

You are simply confirming that the system is ready.

Know Which Account Controls the Phone

If your iPhone goes missing, you need your Apple Account.

If your Android phone goes missing, you need the correct Google Account.

That sounds obvious.

But many people have:

  • Several email addresses

  • Old Google accounts

  • Work accounts

  • Personal accounts

Know which account is tied to the device before the trip.

6. Prepare for Account Recovery Without Your Phone

This is one of the most overlooked parts of travel phone preparation.

Imagine your phone is stolen.

You borrow another device and try to log into your account.

Then the service says:

Enter the verification code sent to your phone.

The missing phone is the device you need in order to recover the missing phone.

That is a bad position to be in.

Check Your Two-Factor Authentication Methods

Review important accounts such as:

  • Apple or Google

  • Main email

  • Password manager

Ask:

If this phone disappeared today, could I still sign in?

That question matters more than whether you can sign in comfortably while the phone is in your hand.

Google Users Should Consider Backup Codes

Google allows users with 2-Step Verification to generate one-time backup codes for situations where they cannot use their normal second step.

Google specifically mentions losing a phone or changing a phone number as situations where backup codes can help.

Store them securely somewhere that does not depend on access to the lost phone.

Do not share them.

Apple Users Can Review Recovery Options

Apple allows users to add trusted recovery contacts who can help provide a recovery code if the user becomes locked out of the Apple Account.

Apple says a recovery contact cannot access the account itself; their role is to help with the recovery process.

You do not have to use this option.

The important point is to understand your recovery setup before something goes wrong.

Make Sure Trusted Phone Numbers Are Current

If an account still lists a phone number you stopped using years ago, fix it before travel.

Check:

  • Trusted phone number

  • Recovery email

  • Recovery contact

  • Other approved authentication methods

A recovery method only helps if you can still access it.

7. Know What You Would Do If the Phone Were Stolen

You do not need to expect theft.

You should know the first steps anyway.

For an iPhone, Apple currently recommends marking a stolen device as lost as quickly as possible. The web Find Devices interface can be used even when the stolen iPhone was the trusted device.

For Android, Find Hub can help locate, secure, or erase a missing device when the required account and device conditions are met.

Before travel, I would know:

  • How to access the finding service

  • Which account I need

  • How to contact my carrier

  • Where important recovery information is stored

You do not need to memorize every recovery step.

You simply need a route back into your accounts.

8. Decide How Your Phone Will Get Mobile Data Abroad

Do not wait until landing to start thinking about connectivity.

Your main options may include:

  • Home-carrier roaming

  • Travel eSIM

  • Local eSIM

  • Local physical SIM

  • Wi-Fi only

The best option depends on:

  • Phone compatibility

  • Destination

  • Trip length

  • Data use

  • Whether you need a local number

If you have not made that decision yet, eSIM vs Physical SIM for International Travel ↗ covers those options in detail.

For phone preparation, the important thing is to know which line will provide your data before you begin changing cellular settings.

9. Check Whether Your Phone Is Carrier Unlocked

If you plan to use another mobile provider abroad, this matters.

A carrier-locked phone may reject another provider's:

  • Physical SIM

  • eSIM

even if the phone technically supports that SIM format.

Apple's current international eSIM guidance, for example, notes that using another carrier requires an unlocked iPhone.

If you bought the phone:

  • Through a carrier

  • On installments

  • Through a subsidized plan

check its lock status before buying a foreign plan.

Do not assume.

Solve Carrier Locking Before You Leave

If the phone is locked, contact the home carrier.

Unlocking policies can depend on:

  • Payment status

  • Contract

  • Device eligibility

  • Carrier rules

That is much easier to deal with at home than after purchasing a travel SIM that the phone refuses to use.

10. Understand Your Roaming Settings Before Departure

Roaming settings are one of the easiest places to create an expensive mistake.

If your normal line should not use international mobile data, make sure you understand how to stop it.

For iPhone, Apple notes that data roaming can create roaming charges outside the home carrier's covered region and allows the setting to be controlled per line on Dual SIM devices.

Android settings vary more by manufacturer, but the same principle applies:

Know which SIM is providing data and what your carrier charges abroad.

Do Not Follow One Universal “Turn Roaming Off” Rule

This can become confusing with travel eSIMs.

Your:

home line

may need roaming disabled to avoid charges.

Your:

travel eSIM

may require data roaming because it connects through partner networks.

Follow the instructions for the exact plan you are using.

The important thing is knowing which line you are changing.

11. Label Dual SIM Lines Clearly

If your phone supports Dual SIM and you are keeping both:

  • Home line

  • Travel line

active, give them clear labels.

For example:

HOME

TRAVEL

Apple currently allows Dual SIM users to label plans and separately choose the cellular-data and default voice lines.

Clear labels make it harder to select the expensive line by mistake.

Choose the Mobile-Data Line Deliberately

If your travel plan is meant to handle mobile data, make sure that line is selected.

Then review any setting that allows automatic cellular-data switching.

You do not want the phone silently moving to your home carrier's expensive roaming service simply because the travel connection becomes weak.

Check Voice and SMS Separately

Your data line and phone-number line do not have to be the same on supported Dual SIM phones.

You may want:

Travel line: data

Home line: calls or SMS

This can help when you need verification texts on your normal number.

But keeping the home line available does not make international calls or SMS free.

Check the home carrier's rates.

12. Test Your Cellular Setup Before the Trip Where Possible

You may not be able to connect a destination eSIM to its foreign network while still at home.

But you can still confirm:

  • The plan appears in settings.

  • You know its label.

  • You understand when it activates.

  • You know which line will handle data.

  • You know whether roaming is required.

  • You have the provider's support details.

That is enough to remove much of the confusion after landing.

Do Not Delete an eSIM as Your First Troubleshooting Step

If an installed travel eSIM does not immediately behave as expected, do not automatically delete it.

Some eSIM activation details cannot simply be reused.

Start with the provider's troubleshooting instructions instead.

Your goal before travel is to understand the setup, not repeatedly rebuild it.

My First-Stage Phone Preparation Checklist

Before moving on to offline maps, downloaded information, battery settings, and arrival-day setup, I would make sure these basics are complete:

Phone

  • Operating system updated

  • Important apps updated

  • Enough free storage

  • Strong screen lock enabled

  • Biometrics working

Backup

  • Fresh device backup completed

  • Photos protected

  • Important app data checked

Recovery

  • Find My or Find Hub enabled

  • Lost-device service tested

  • Main account password known or securely stored

  • Recovery information current

  • Backup authentication method available

Mobile Service

  • Connectivity option chosen

  • Carrier lock status checked

  • Travel SIM/eSIM compatibility confirmed

  • Home roaming costs understood

  • Correct line selected for data

  • Dual SIM lines clearly labeled

At this point, the phone is much better prepared for the two problems that cause the most stress abroad:

losing connectivity

and:

losing access to the phone itself.

13. Download Maps Before You Leave

Even if you plan to have mobile data throughout the trip, I would still download the important areas before departure.

Mobile service can disappear in:

  • Underground stations

  • Rural areas

  • Parking garages

  • Border regions

  • Large buildings

You may also have a few hours after landing before your travel data starts working properly.

Offline maps give you another way to reach:

  • Hotel

  • Airport

  • Train station

  • Rental-car office

  • Important attractions

without depending completely on the network.

Download More Than Just the Hotel Area

If I were visiting Paris, I would not download only the few streets around my hotel.

I would include enough area to cover:

  • Accommodation

  • Airport or arrival station

  • Main sightseeing areas

  • Important transport connections

For a road trip, download the route and surrounding areas rather than individual towns only.

Google Maps Has Important Offline Limits

Google Maps currently lets Android and iPhone users download selected areas before traveling.

When completely offline, downloaded Google Maps can provide driving navigation when the full route falls inside the downloaded area.

However, offline Google Maps does not provide normal:

  • Public transport directions

  • Walking directions

  • Cycling directions

  • Live traffic

  • Alternative driving routes

while disconnected.

That distinction matters.

Downloading a city does not mean every Google Maps feature will behave exactly as it does online.

Apple Maps Offers Broader Offline Directions in Supported Areas

On supported iPhones and regions, Apple Maps offline downloads currently include:

  • Driving directions

  • Walking directions

  • Cycling directions

  • Public transport directions

  • Estimated arrival times

  • Some place information

without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Coverage still varies by location.

Check your destination before assuming offline maps are available everywhere.

Download Maps While You Have Wi-Fi

Offline maps can take significant storage.

Before departure:

  • Connect to Wi-Fi.

  • Download the areas.

  • Check the storage size.

  • Open them once.

  • Test them in airplane mode.

That final test is important.

Do not simply see the word Downloaded and assume everything you need will work.

Update Old Offline Maps

Downloaded map information can become outdated.

Google Maps can automatically update downloaded areas when connected to Wi-Fi, and Apple Maps also provides automatic-update controls for offline maps.

If you downloaded a destination months ago, check it again shortly before traveling.

Save Important Places Separately

I also save key locations such as:

  • Hotel

  • Airport

  • Main station

  • Car rental office

  • Meeting point

inside the map app.

This reduces searching after landing.

For a deeper comparison of which map app to use for walking, driving, transit, and offline navigation, see Best Navigation Apps for Travelers ↗.

14. Prepare Translation for Offline Use

If you are traveling somewhere you do not speak the local language, download the relevant translation language before departure.

Google Translate currently allows supported languages to be downloaded for offline translation. Some downloaded languages can also support camera-based translation without an internet connection.

On iPhone, Apple's Translate app also allows supported languages to be downloaded for on-device use. Apple notes that both the input and output languages should be downloaded for offline translation.

Test the Exact Feature You Expect to Use

Do not only test:

Hello → local language

Also check whether you can use:

  • Typed text

  • Camera translation

  • Voice

  • Conversation mode

without internet.

Offline feature support can be different from normal connected translation.

Download Both Sides of the Conversation

If your app requires it, make sure you have:

your language

and:

destination language

available offline.

It is easy to download only one and discover the missing language after landing.

Save a Few Important Phrases Separately

I would keep a few essential phrases outside the translation app too.

For example:

  • I have a reservation.

  • Please take me to this address.

  • I need help.

  • I do not understand.

Important allergy or medical information deserves even more care and should not depend only on automatic translation.

For the differences between Google Translate, Apple Translate, DeepL, Papago, and other options, see Best Translation Apps for International Travel ↗.

15. Save Your Accommodation Information Offline

Do not make your first night depend on having mobile data.

I keep the accommodation's:

  • Name

  • Full address

  • Phone number

  • Check-in instructions

  • Booking reference

somewhere accessible offline.

The original local-language address can be particularly useful when showing it to:

  • Taxi driver

  • Station employee

  • Local person

Keep the Exact Address, Not Only a Map Pin

A saved pin is useful.

A written address is another backup.

If one app stops working, you can still:

  • Show the address

  • Copy it

  • Enter it into another map

  • Give it to a driver

For countries using a different writing system, I would keep the local-script version too.

16. Save Important Reservations for Offline Access

Your phone may hold:

  • Flight booking

  • Hotel reservation

  • Train ticket

  • Bus ticket

  • Tour confirmation

  • Rental-car booking

Do not assume every booking app will load perfectly without internet.

Before departure, open the important reservations and make sure you know how you will access them offline.

Add Boarding Passes to Your Phone Wallet Where Supported

Apple Wallet and Google Wallet can make boarding passes easier to access without searching through email.

Google says saved boarding passes can remain accessible without an internet connection after they have been added to Google Wallet.

Wallet support depends on:

  • Airline

  • Airport

  • Ticket type

  • Phone

So check the individual booking.

Keep a Backup of Important Tickets

For a normal static ticket, a downloaded PDF or screenshot can sometimes be useful as a backup.

But do not assume a screenshot will always work.

Some tickets use:

  • Changing barcodes

  • Dynamic QR codes

  • App-based validation

that require the original ticket or wallet pass.

If the provider says you must use its app, follow that instruction.

Open the Ticket Before Reaching the Gate

I do not like discovering at the front of a queue that:

  • The app logged me out.

  • The ticket needs downloading.

  • The account wants another verification step.

Open important tickets earlier while you still have a good connection.

17. Keep Important Travel Documents Accessible

Your phone can be useful for storing reference copies of documents such as:

  • Passport information page

  • Visa confirmation

  • Insurance information

  • Driver-related documents

  • Booking confirmations

But I would not make the phone the only place these documents exist.

Depending on the trip, you may also keep copies:

  • In secure cloud storage

  • With another trusted device

  • In a safe physical backup

where appropriate.

A Digital Copy Does Not Automatically Replace the Original

Having a passport photograph on your phone does not mean an immigration officer will accept it instead of the physical passport.

The same principle can apply to:

  • Visa documents

  • Driving permits

  • Insurance paperwork

Use digital copies as convenient references or backups where appropriate.

Carry the officially required original document when the rules require it.

18. Organize Travel Files Into One Place

You do not want to search through hundreds of downloads while standing at immigration or hotel reception.

I prefer creating one simple folder for the trip.

For example:

Japan 2027

Inside:

  • Flight

  • Hotels

  • Insurance

  • Train bookings

  • Important confirmations

The exact app does not matter.

The point is knowing where everything is.

Use Clear File Names

Instead of:

IMG_02983.pdf

rename important files to something like:

Tokyo Hotel Confirmation

or:

Return Flight Booking

A few seconds of organization at home can save frustration during the trip.

19. Prepare Mobile Payments, but Keep a Backup

Phone payments can make travel easier in destinations where contactless payment is common.

Before departure, check:

  • Whether your card is already in the phone wallet

  • Whether the card is still valid

  • Whether your bank supports mobile payments

  • Whether contactless payments are commonly accepted at the destination

Google notes that payment-card support depends on the country and the individual bank.

Apple Pay availability also depends on participating countries, banks, and card issuers.

Do Not Make the Phone Your Only Payment Method

Even if mobile payments work perfectly, I would still carry another payment option.

Possible problems include:

  • Phone battery dies.

  • Shop accepts physical card but not your wallet.

  • Bank blocks a transaction.

  • Terminal does not support contactless payment.

A phone wallet is a convenience layer.

It should not become your entire travel-payment plan.

20. Check Your Banking Apps Before Departure

Open the banking apps you may need.

Confirm:

  • You can sign in.

  • Biometrics work.

  • The app is updated.

  • Your phone number and account details are current.

Do this before leaving your normal environment.

Some financial apps can become more cautious when they detect:

  • New country

  • New network

  • Unusual transaction

You do not want to discover a forgotten password while trying to pay for a hotel.

Understand How Your Bank Handles International Use

Some banks allow international card controls inside the app.

Others may use automatic fraud systems.

Check your own provider rather than following an old universal rule that every traveler must “notify the bank.”

What matters is knowing:

  • Whether international use is enabled

  • How to contact the bank

  • What to do if the card is blocked

21. Make Emergency Information Easy to Find

Your phone may support emergency information that can be viewed without fully unlocking the device.

On supported devices, this can include things such as:

  • Emergency contacts

  • Medical information

The exact setup varies between iPhone manufacturers and Android devices.

If you choose to use these features, make sure the information is:

  • Current

  • Accurate

  • Something you are comfortable making accessible from the lock screen

Save Important Numbers Separately

I would also keep important numbers such as:

  • Travel insurer

  • Bank

  • Mobile provider

  • Accommodation

  • Emergency contact

in an accessible place.

Do not depend entirely on searching the web after something goes wrong.

22. Prepare the Phone for Longer Battery Life

Travel often uses more battery than a normal day at home.

Your phone may be doing:

  • Navigation

  • Photography

  • Mobile data

  • Translation

  • Ticket display

  • Messaging

  • Ride booking

for hours at a time.

That means battery preparation matters even if the phone normally lasts all day.

Check Battery Health Before a Long Trip

If your phone battery already struggles to reach the afternoon at home, international travel will probably make the problem more noticeable.

On current iPhones, battery-health information is available in the Battery settings, although the exact path varies by model. Apple recommends considering service when the phone indicates that battery service is needed.

Android battery-health information varies more by manufacturer.

If the battery is already failing badly, solve that before relying on it abroad.

23. Know How to Turn On Battery-Saving Mode

You should not have to search through settings when the battery reaches 12%.

iPhone

Low Power Mode reduces background activity and other power use to extend battery life. Newer supported iPhones also offer Adaptive Power, which can make automatic adjustments based on usage.

Android

Android's Battery Saver can reduce or delay background activity and some network or visual functions to extend battery life. The exact controls vary by manufacturer.

Know where the setting is before travel.

Battery Saver Can Affect Apps

Power-saving modes may delay:

  • Background refresh

  • Email

  • Some network activity

  • App updates

That is normally acceptable when you are trying to keep the phone alive.

But if you are waiting for an important live update, remember that aggressive battery saving can change app behavior.

24. Reduce Screen Power Use

The screen can consume a significant amount of battery.

Simple changes can help:

  • Reduce brightness

  • Use automatic brightness where useful

  • Let the screen turn off sooner

  • Avoid leaving navigation or video running unnecessarily

You do not need to make the screen so dark that you cannot read it.

The goal is simply to avoid wasting power.

25. Watch Apps That Work Constantly in the Background

Some apps may use:

  • Location

  • Mobile data

  • Background refresh

even when you are not actively looking at them.

Review apps that have no reason to continuously work during the trip.

Possible examples include:

  • Old social apps

  • Shopping apps

  • Games

  • Apps you do not use anymore

I would not disable important travel functions just to save a tiny amount of battery.

Focus on unnecessary background activity.

26. Be Careful With Continuous Location Access

Several travel apps need location access.

That may include:

  • Maps

  • Ride-hailing

  • Weather

  • Camera location tagging

But every app does not need permanent location access.

Review permissions and choose the level appropriate for how you actually use each app.

For many apps:

While Using the App

may make more sense than unrestricted continuous access.

The exact options depend on your operating system.

27. Control Cloud Photo Uploads on Mobile Data

Travelers can create huge numbers of photos and videos.

If your photo service automatically uploads everything over mobile data, it can consume a limited travel plan quickly.

Review:

  • Mobile-data uploads

  • Video uploads

  • Background syncing

before departure.

A practical setup may be:

Take photos all day → upload automatically when connected to trusted Wi-Fi

if that fits your backup needs.

The right choice depends on how much mobile data you have.

28. Download Entertainment Before the Flight

If you want:

  • Movies

  • TV

  • Podcasts

  • Music

  • Audiobooks

download them while connected to Wi-Fi.

This saves:

  • Mobile data

  • Airport Wi-Fi dependence

  • Battery used by constant streaming

It also means you still have entertainment if the aircraft Wi-Fi does not work.

Check That the Download Really Works Offline

Some streaming apps require the device to reconnect periodically.

Before leaving home, open the app in airplane mode and make sure the downloaded content appears.

This is especially useful before a very long flight.

29. Review Which Apps Are Allowed to Use Mobile Data

A travel data package may be much smaller than your home plan.

You probably do not want limited international data being consumed by:

  • Automatic app updates

  • Large cloud backups

  • High-quality video

  • Background downloads

Review mobile-data permissions before departure.

You can always re-enable something when needed.

Use Wi-Fi for Large Downloads

Whenever practical, use reliable Wi-Fi for:

  • App updates

  • Offline maps

  • Language packs

  • Movies

  • Large file uploads

Do these tasks at home rather than using the travel plan after arrival.

30. Prepare Messaging Apps Before Changing SIMs

If you use apps such as:

  • WhatsApp

  • Signal

  • Other account-based messengers

make sure they are already working before travel.

Changing which SIM provides mobile data does not necessarily mean you need to change the phone number registered with every messaging app.

Avoid making unnecessary account changes simply because you installed a travel SIM.

Keep Important Contacts Saved Properly

Make sure essential contacts are stored in a synced account rather than only somewhere that could disappear with a SIM.

Examples:

  • Family

  • Hotel

  • Tour operator

  • Travel partner

  • Emergency contact

That way, replacing a SIM does not remove your important contact information.

31. Set Automatic Date and Time

Crossing time zones can affect:

  • Alarms

  • Calendar events

  • Boarding reminders

  • Reservation times

I normally keep automatic date and time enabled so the phone can adjust when it connects in the new location.

After landing, confirm that:

  • Local time looks correct.

  • Calendar appointments still appear at the expected time.

  • Alarms are set for the time you intend.

Pay extra attention when a booking was originally entered in another time zone.

32. Add Useful Controls to the Quick Settings Area

Before travel, I like knowing how to quickly reach:

  • Airplane mode

  • Mobile data

  • Wi-Fi

  • Bluetooth

  • Battery saver

  • Flashlight

  • Screen brightness

On iPhone, this is typically through Control Center.

On Android, it is usually through Quick Settings.

Arrange the controls you use most so you are not digging through menus in an airport.

33. Make the Home Screen Easier to Use During the Trip

You do not need a completely new phone layout.

But placing important apps together can help.

For example, one travel folder could contain:

  • Airline

  • Maps

  • Translation

  • Booking

  • Local transport

  • Wallet

Then you know where everything is.

This can be especially useful when you are:

  • Tired

  • Carrying luggage

  • Standing in a queue

Remove Apps You Will Never Use From the Main Screen

You do not need to uninstall them.

Simply make the important travel tools easier to reach.

Fast access matters more than making the phone look organized.

34. Test the Phone in a “No Internet” Situation

This is one of the best preparation exercises.

Before departure:

  • Turn on airplane mode.

  • Leave Wi-Fi off.

  • Pretend you just landed with no service.

Now try to:

  • Find your hotel address.

  • Open your flight booking.

  • Open your map.

  • Translate a basic phrase.

  • View an important document.

  • Find an emergency contact.

Every failure you discover at home is something you can fix before the trip.

Then Test the “Low Battery” Situation

Imagine the phone has 15% battery.

Do you know:

  • Where Battery Saver is?

  • Where the power bank is?

  • Which cable charges the phone?

The solution should be obvious.

If you need five minutes to work it out at home, simplify your setup.

Your Second-Stage Phone Preparation Checklist

Offline Access

  • Destination maps downloaded

  • Maps tested in airplane mode

  • Translation languages downloaded

  • Offline translation tested

  • Accommodation address saved

  • Original local address saved where useful

Bookings

  • Flight details available offline

  • Hotel confirmations saved

  • Train or bus tickets saved

  • Boarding passes added to Wallet where supported

  • Important booking references stored clearly

Documents

  • Important travel files organized

  • Secure backup available

  • Original documents packed where required

Payments

  • Mobile wallet checked

  • Banking app login tested

  • Backup payment method available

  • International card settings understood

Battery

  • Battery condition checked

  • Battery-saving mode understood

  • Screen settings reviewed

  • Unnecessary background activity reduced

  • Power bank prepared if needed

Data

  • Large updates completed on Wi-Fi

  • Offline entertainment downloaded

  • Cloud photo settings reviewed

  • Unnecessary mobile-data use limited

Final Test

  • Airplane-mode test completed

  • Important files accessible without internet

  • Home screen organized

  • Quick settings ready

  • Local-time behavior understood

After this stage, the phone should remain useful even if the first few hours abroad do not go exactly as planned.

35. Know What to Check Immediately After Landing

Once you arrive, avoid changing several settings at the same time.

First confirm the basics:

  • Local time is correct.

  • Your travel SIM or roaming plan has connected.

  • Mobile data works.

  • Your home SIM is not using unwanted roaming data.

  • Maps can find your location.

  • Important bookings still open.

If everything works, leave the setup alone.

Travelers sometimes create problems by changing settings simply because they expect something to be wrong.

Give the Phone a Few Minutes to Connect

After landing and leaving airplane mode, your phone may need a little time to register on a local network.

If mobile service does not appear immediately:

  • Wait briefly.

  • Check that the correct SIM is enabled.

  • Confirm mobile data is assigned to the intended line.

  • Check whether the travel SIM requires data roaming.

  • Restart the phone if necessary.

Follow the instructions from your actual mobile provider before changing advanced network settings.

Check Which SIM Is Using Mobile Data

This is particularly important with Dual SIM.

You may want:

HOME: calls and verification texts

TRAVEL: mobile data

Check the setting instead of assuming the phone selected the travel line automatically.

A small mistake here can create unnecessary roaming charges.

36. Check Data Use During the First Day

I like checking mobile-data use after the first several hours abroad.

This helps identify apps that may be consuming far more data than expected.

Possible causes include:

  • Cloud photo uploads

  • Video streaming

  • Automatic downloads

  • Social-media video

  • App updates

  • Hotspot use

If something is using too much data, adjust it early rather than discovering the problem after the allowance is gone.

Do Not Obsess Over Every Megabyte

The goal is not to constantly monitor the phone.

Check enough to make sure the setup behaves normally.

Once you understand your usage, let the phone do its job.

37. Be Careful With Public Wi-Fi

Hotels, airports, cafés, and stations often provide Wi-Fi.

That can be useful when:

  • Mobile service is weak.

  • You want to save data.

  • You need a large download.

But I would not connect automatically to every network with a familiar name.

Check that you are joining the correct network.

For example, ask hotel staff for the official Wi-Fi name rather than choosing the first network containing the hotel's name.

HTTPS Has Improved Public Wi-Fi Safety

Modern websites and apps commonly encrypt traffic using HTTPS, so public Wi-Fi should not be treated as automatically dangerous in the way older travel advice sometimes suggests.

Still, shared networks can create privacy and network-trust concerns.

For sensitive tasks, I prefer:

  • My own mobile data

or:

  • A trusted connection

when practical.

A VPN Is an Optional Additional Privacy Layer

Some travelers use a VPN on:

  • Hotel Wi-Fi

  • Airport Wi-Fi

  • Café networks

to reduce what the local network can observe about their internet traffic.

A VPN is not required for every traveler and does not make unsafe websites or scams safe.

If you want to decide whether you need one, Best VPNs for Travelers: ↗ When You Need One and What to Look For covers that separately.

Complete Captive-Portal Login Before Troubleshooting

Some hotel or airport Wi-Fi requires you to open a browser and accept:

  • Terms

  • Room details

  • Email

  • Access page

before normal internet works.

If your phone says:

Connected but no internet

check whether a sign-in page is waiting.

Do this before assuming the phone or VPN is broken.

38. Turn Off Automatic Wi-Fi Joining Where It Creates Problems

Phones can remember networks from previous visits.

That is convenient at home.

During travel, I do not want the phone constantly connecting to weak or unwanted networks simply because they are available.

Review automatic connection behavior if you notice the phone repeatedly leaving working mobile data for poor public Wi-Fi.

The exact controls differ between iPhone and Android.

39. Be Careful With Unknown QR Codes

QR codes are common while traveling.

You may use them for:

  • Menus

  • Tickets

  • Transport

  • Hotel information

  • Payments

Most are harmless and useful.

But a QR code can also send you to an unexpected website.

Before:

  • Entering a password

  • Making a payment

  • Downloading an app

check that the destination looks legitimate.

A QR code is simply another way of opening a link.

It does not make the destination automatically trustworthy.

40. Install Apps From Official App Stores

You may encounter signs or messages telling you to install an app for:

  • Transport

  • Hotel access

  • Local services

When possible, search for the official app through:

  • Apple App Store

  • Google Play

rather than installing software from an unfamiliar download link.

Check:

  • Developer name

  • Reviews

  • Official website

if you are unsure.

Avoid Installing Random “Required” Apps Under Pressure

If someone says you must immediately install an unfamiliar app to:

  • Receive a refund

  • Verify a payment

  • Fix your bank account

  • Release a booking

be cautious.

Travel creates situations where people are:

  • Tired

  • Distracted

  • In a hurry

which can make scams easier.

41. Review App Permissions

Travel apps may request access to:

  • Location

  • Camera

  • Microphone

  • Photos

  • Contacts

  • Bluetooth

Some permissions are necessary.

A navigation app needs location.

A translation app may need the microphone.

A QR scanner may need the camera.

But an app should not automatically receive everything.

Grant permissions based on the feature you actually use.

Remove Temporary Permissions After the Trip

You may install several destination-specific apps during travel.

After returning home, review apps you no longer need.

You can:

  • Remove them

  • Revoke permissions

  • Cancel subscriptions

instead of letting unused travel apps remain indefinitely.

42. Be Careful With Public USB Charging

If I have my own charger and cable, I prefer plugging into a normal electrical outlet.

Public USB ports may be:

  • Slow

  • Damaged

  • Unreliable

The easier solution is usually:

your own charger + wall outlet

or:

your own power bank.

You do not need to become afraid of every USB socket.

The point is that carrying your own charging setup gives you more control.

43. Keep the Phone Physically Secure

Digital preparation helps, but physical habits matter too.

Be more careful with your phone in:

  • Crowded public transport

  • Busy tourist areas

  • Outdoor café tables

  • Station platforms

Avoid leaving it:

  • Unattended while charging

  • On the edge of a restaurant table

  • In an open backpack pocket

A strong password cannot stop someone from physically taking the device.

Avoid Holding the Phone Carelessly Near Traffic

Navigation often means looking at a phone while walking.

Before focusing on the map:

  • Move away from the road.

  • Stop somewhere safe.

  • Check the route.

  • Put the phone away when appropriate.

This helps with both personal safety and device security.

44. Know What to Do If Your Phone Is Lost

If you cannot find the phone, first determine whether it is:

  • Misplaced nearby

  • Left somewhere

  • Probably stolen

Use your lost-device service from another trusted device.

Depending on the phone, you may be able to:

  • Play a sound

  • Check location

  • Mark it as lost

  • Display contact information

  • Lock it

  • Erase it if recovery becomes unlikely

The exact steps differ between Apple and Android.

The important part is that you prepared access to these tools before traveling.

If the Phone Is Stolen, Protect Accounts Too

Depending on the situation, you may also need to:

  • Contact the mobile carrier.

  • Suspend the SIM.

  • Contact financial providers.

  • Change important credentials.

  • Report the theft locally where appropriate.

Do not rush to erase a phone before considering whether doing so will affect your ability to locate it.

Follow the lost-device guidance for your specific platform.

45. Keep Your Phone's Device Information Somewhere Else

It can be useful to keep important device information outside the phone.

Depending on your device, that may include:

  • Model

  • Serial number

  • IMEI

You do not need to carry these details openly in your wallet.

Store them securely somewhere you can access if the phone disappears.

They may be useful when dealing with:

  • Carrier

  • Insurance

  • Police report

depending on the circumstances.

46. Do Not Store Every Sensitive Document in an Unlocked Photo Album

Travelers often photograph:

  • Passport

  • Visa

  • Credit card

  • Insurance documents

and leave everything inside the normal photo gallery.

That is convenient but may expose more information than necessary if someone gains access to the phone.

For sensitive files, consider a more secure storage method available on your device or trusted service.

Also ask whether you need the copy at all.

Store only what helps the trip.

47. Do Not Save Card PINs or Passwords in Notes

Avoid keeping information such as:

Bank card PIN: 1234

or:

Email password: example123

inside an ordinary notes file.

Use a proper password manager or other secure method.

Your phone can already unlock access to many important services.

Do not make it easier for someone who gets into the device.

48. A Password Manager Can Make Travel Recovery Easier

A password manager can help you access important accounts without memorizing every password.

This can be especially useful if you need to sign in from:

  • Replacement phone

  • Laptop

  • Another trusted device

However, make sure you understand how to access the password manager if your main phone disappears.

If the password manager's only second-factor method lives on the missing phone, recovery can still become difficult.

Test your recovery path before departure.

49. Prepare for Verification Codes

International travel can create problems with services that insist on SMS verification.

If you need your home number for:

  • Banking

  • Email

  • Account recovery

think about how you will receive those texts.

If you keep the home SIM active, understand whether:

  • Receiving SMS costs anything

  • Calls cost extra

  • Mobile data is disabled on that line

Check with your carrier because policies differ.

Do Not Change Important Account Phone Numbers Without a Reason

Installing a travel eSIM does not normally mean you must replace the phone number attached to:

  • Bank

  • Email

  • Messaging app

Unnecessary account changes can create more problems than they solve.

50. Prepare for a Dead Phone

Even with perfect battery preparation, the phone can eventually reach 0%.

Ask yourself:

What would I lose immediately if the phone died?

Possible answers:

  • Hotel address

  • Train ticket

  • Boarding pass

  • Payment access

  • Contact details

For the most important items, keep another route to the information.

That might mean:

  • Paper copy

  • Travel partner's phone

  • Laptop

  • Secure cloud copy

You do not need paper copies of everything.

Focus on what could stop the trip.

51. Share Critical Information With Your Travel Partner

If two people are traveling together, do not keep:

  • All hotel bookings

  • Every ticket

  • Insurance information

only on one person's phone.

Both travelers can keep access to the essentials.

That creates an easy backup if:

  • One phone dies.

  • One phone is lost.

  • The travelers temporarily separate.

Do Not Share More Than Necessary

Backup does not mean sending every sensitive document to everyone.

Share only what the other traveler genuinely needs.

52. Keep Your Phone Usable During Border and Airport Travel

Airports can involve long periods without easy access to:

  • Charger

  • Wi-Fi

  • Mobile signal

Before leaving for the airport:

  • Charge the phone.

  • Charge the power bank.

  • Download boarding information.

  • Keep the correct cable accessible.

Do not begin a long travel day at 34% battery simply because there are outlets at the airport.

Some may be:

  • Occupied

  • Broken

  • Far from your gate

Start with your own battery full.

53. Do Not Depend Completely on Airport Wi-Fi

You may need internet after landing for:

  • Ride booking

  • Messages

  • Hotel check-in

Airport Wi-Fi often works.

But it can also be:

  • Slow

  • Busy

  • Difficult to authenticate

This is another reason I prefer having the connectivity setup decided before departure.

Wi-Fi can then be a useful option rather than your entire arrival plan.

54. Check Your Phone Before Leaving the Airport

Before you leave the arrival terminal, confirm that you can:

  • Use mobile data

  • Access maps

  • Find the accommodation

  • Contact someone if needed

The airport is often easier for solving connection problems than discovering them later:

  • On a motorway

  • At night

  • Outside a closed train station

If something is wrong, deal with it while you still have:

  • Airport Wi-Fi

  • Seating

  • Staff

  • Time

where possible.

55. Keep Your Phone Setup Simple During the Trip

Once everything works, stop changing settings unnecessarily.

The phone does not need constant optimization.

A good travel setup should mostly disappear into the background.

Use it when you need:

  • Map

  • Ticket

  • Translation

  • Communication

and enjoy the trip.

Download and test at home.

Leave room for photos, maps, and files.

Set it up before the phone disappears.

Carry a backup payment method.

Only install tools that solve a real problem.

Review permissions.

Confirm the correct network.

Use only the information needed.

A backup that disappears with the phone is not much of a backup.

Final Thoughts

In short, A well-prepared travel phone should still be useful when the internet disappears, should not accidentally create roaming charges, and should be recoverable if it is lost. I focus on backup, account recovery, connectivity, offline access, battery, and a few important security settings before adding anything else. Once those basics are tested at home, your phone can quietly support the trip instead of becoming another problem to solve abroad.
