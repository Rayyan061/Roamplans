Best Navigation Apps for Travelers

✓ Practical guidance⌖ International travel✦ Updated August 2026

Finding your way around an unfamiliar destination can become one of the most ↗ stressful parts of a trip. You may need to walk from a train station to your hotel, understand a metro route, drive through an unfamiliar city, or find your way back after spending the day sightseeing. The right navigation app can make these everyday travel moments much easier.

When I prepare for a trip, I usually choose one main navigation app and test it before departure. I save my accommodation, airport, main stations, and other important locations, then download offline maps when the app supports them. I also check what actually works without internet because an offline map does not always include offline walking, public transport, traffic, and every other feature.

For this guide, I compared navigation apps from the point of view of an international traveler. I looked at global coverage, walking routes, public transport, driving, offline use, ease of use, and how practical each app is when you are already at the destination.

!

App Note: Navigation features, transport data, road information, offline support, and regional coverage can change. Check the app on your own destination before depending on it for an important journey.

Key Takeaways

  • Google Maps is my best overall navigation app for most international travelers.

  • Apple Maps is a strong option for iPhone users, especially where its offline map features are supported.

  • HERE WeGo is worth considering when offline navigation is a major priority.

  • Citymapper can be excellent for public transport in cities where it has strong coverage.

  • Organic Maps is a useful free option for travelers who want offline maps and a stronger privacy focus.

  • Waze is best suited to driving and live road conditions rather than general sightseeing.

  • No navigation app has equally strong information in every country or city.

  • Download important maps before departure instead of waiting until you lose mobile service.

  • Test your main airport-to-hotel route before the trip.

  • Keep your accommodation address available separately in case your phone or map app fails.

What Should a Navigation App Do for a Traveler?

For most trips, a navigation app should help answer basic questions quickly.

For example:

  • Where am I?

  • How far is my hotel?

  • Can I walk there?

  • Which train or bus should I take?

  • How long will the journey take?

  • Is there another driving route?

  • Can I still see the map without mobile data?

You may also use the app to find:

  • Restaurants

  • Pharmacies

  • ATMs

  • Fuel stations

  • Attractions

  • Supermarkets

  • Train stations

The most useful app is not necessarily the one with the most features.

It is the one that handles the journeys you expect to make.

International Coverage Matters

For international travel, I prefer an app that can remain useful across several countries.

This matters on trips such as:

France → Switzerland → Italy

or:

Thailand → Cambodia → Vietnam

Changing your entire navigation setup every time you cross a border creates unnecessary work.

That does not mean a global app will be equally good everywhere.

Street information, public transport data, traffic updates, and business listings can vary significantly between destinations.

Before departure, test a few real routes in the places you will visit.

Walking Directions Matter More Than Many Travelers Expect

Walking often becomes one of your main forms of transport while traveling.

You may walk between:

  • Hotel and station

  • Attractions

  • Restaurants

  • Shopping areas

  • Metro stops

  • Museums

A route that looks short on a normal map may involve:

  • Stairs

  • Bridges

  • Indirect streets

  • Pedestrian-only paths

  • Large stations

  • Difficult crossings

A useful walking map can save time and reduce unnecessary backtracking.

Public Transport Can Change the Best App

If most of your trip will involve:

  • Metro

  • Bus

  • Train

  • Tram

  • Ferry

public transport information becomes much more important.

Some apps provide:

  • Route combinations

  • Departure times

  • Transfers

  • Live arrivals

  • Service disruptions

But public transport information varies by city.

An app that works extremely well in London may provide less detail in a smaller destination.

For an important airport or long-distance connection, I would still confirm the current service with the transport operator.

Driving Requires Different Features

If you are renting a car, you may care more about:

  • Turn-by-turn navigation

  • Traffic

  • Road closures

  • Alternative routes

  • Lane guidance

  • Arrival time

A good city public transport app may be almost useless for that trip.

This is why I would not choose one navigation app simply because it ranks first on a generic list.

Start with your main transport type.

Offline Navigation Can Be Very Important Abroad

Mobile data can stop working at inconvenient times.

You may experience:

  • Poor rural signal

  • No underground reception

  • Roaming problems

  • eSIM activation problems

  • Limited mobile data

Downloading maps before departure gives you another option.

However, you need to know what offline actually means in each app.

Some apps may still provide full route guidance.

Others may only let you view the downloaded map or use certain transport modes.

Test it before leaving.

How I Test an Offline Map

A simple test is to download the destination and then put the phone into airplane mode.

Try:

  • Opening the map

  • Searching for your hotel

  • Starting a route

  • Changing the route

  • Checking walking directions

  • Checking public transport if the app claims to support it offline

This tells you what your phone can actually do without internet.

It is much better to discover a limitation at home than outside an airport.

1. Google Maps — Best Overall Navigation App for Most Travelers

Best for: General international travel, walking, driving, finding places, and public transport in supported destinations.

Platforms: Android, iPhone, iPad, and web.

My verdict: If I could choose only one general navigation app for a normal international trip, Google Maps would usually be my first choice.

Google Maps combines many everyday travel needs inside one familiar app.

Depending on the destination, you can use it for:

  • Walking

  • Driving

  • Public transport

  • Cycling

  • Finding businesses

  • Locating attractions

  • Finding accommodation

  • Saving places

  • Downloading map areas

Its main advantage is versatility.

You can use the same app after landing to find your hotel, check tomorrow's walking route, locate a restaurant, and see how to reach the main train station.

Why Google Maps Works Well for International Travel

Imagine arriving somewhere you have never visited.

Within the first few hours, you may need to find:

  • The airport exit

  • A train or bus connection

  • Your accommodation

  • Somewhere to eat

  • A nearby supermarket or ATM

Google Maps can often help with all of those.

That makes it a practical starting point for beginners who want one main map rather than several specialist tools.

Google Maps Supports Offline Map Downloads

Google Maps allows you to download selected areas to your phone before the trip.

This is useful when:

  • Mobile data is unavailable

  • Your travel SIM has not connected

  • Reception becomes weak

  • You are driving through a remote area

  • You want to reduce mobile-data use

I would download the destination while connected to reliable Wi-Fi.

Do not wait until you are already abroad and struggling with a weak connection.

Google Maps Has Important Offline Limits

This is one of the most important things to know before depending on Google Maps offline.

Its offline mode is much stronger for driving than for other transport types.

When completely offline, you should not expect the normal full experience for:

  • Walking directions

  • Public transport directions

  • Cycling directions

Offline driving also loses live features such as:

  • Traffic information

  • Alternative routes based on current conditions

The downloaded map is still useful.

You just need to know that offline map and complete offline navigation for every travel mode are not the same thing.

Download Enough Area

Do not download only the streets around your hotel.

Think about where the trip actually begins and where you may travel.

Include areas around:

  • Airport

  • Accommodation

  • Main train station

  • Sightseeing areas

  • Day-trip destinations

  • Rental-car routes

For example, if your airport sits far outside the city, a city-center download may not cover your first journey.

Check Offline Maps Before Departure

If you download a destination far in advance, check it again shortly before leaving.

Offline map files can need updates.

This also gives you a chance to make sure:

  • The download still exists

  • Your destination is completely covered

  • You have enough phone storage

  • You understand where to find offline maps in the app

Google Maps Is Very Useful for Walking With Mobile Data

Walking directions are one of the reasons I find Google Maps practical for city travel.

You can quickly compare:

Walk: 12 minutes

against:

Public transport: 18 minutes

That can stop you from waiting for a bus when the attraction is actually close enough to walk.

Walking directions also help you understand the real street layout.

Two places may look close on the map but require a longer route because of:

  • Railway tracks

  • Rivers

  • Major roads

  • Limited crossings

Public Transport Is Strong in Many Cities

Google Maps provides public transport directions in many destinations.

Depending on the city, it may show:

  • Metro routes

  • Bus routes

  • Trains

  • Trams

  • Ferries

  • Transfer points

  • Departure information

However, the level of detail can vary.

A major city may provide detailed live data, while another destination may show more basic scheduled information.

For a normal sightseeing journey, this may be enough.

For an airport train or another connection you cannot afford to miss, check the operator's current information too.

Google Maps Is Useful for Finding Nearby Places

Travel navigation is not always about reaching a famous attraction.

Sometimes you need:

pharmacy near me

ATM near hotel

supermarket nearby

fuel station

restaurant open now

Google Maps has a large database of businesses and places, which makes these searches convenient.

For important details such as:

  • Attraction opening hours

  • Official ticket availability

  • Entry requirements

verify the information with the actual business or attraction before relying on it.

Saved Places Can Make Navigation Easier

You can save locations you expect to use repeatedly.

I normally consider saving:

  • Accommodation

  • Airport

  • Main station

  • Car-rental office

  • Important attraction

  • Tour meeting point

This reduces repeated searching.

It also makes it easier to quickly open a route when you are tired or in a hurry.

Google Maps Pros

  • Strong general international usefulness

  • Works on Android and iPhone

  • Walking directions

  • Driving navigation

  • Public transport in many destinations

  • Large database of businesses and attractions

  • Offline map downloads

  • Familiar interface

  • Useful for mixed travel styles

Google Maps Limitations

  • Full walking navigation does not work in normal offline mode.

  • Offline public transport directions are limited.

  • Offline driving loses live traffic information.

  • Transit quality varies by destination.

  • Offline map availability and detail can vary by region.

Who Should Choose Google Maps?

Google Maps is my first choice if:

  • You want one main navigation app.

  • You will combine walking and public transport.

  • You want to find businesses and attractions as well as routes.

  • You use Android.

  • You travel internationally.

  • You expect to have mobile data for the full navigation experience.

For many beginner travelers, this is enough as the main map.

Add another navigation app only when your destination or transport style gives you a clear reason.

2. Apple Maps — Best Navigation App for iPhone Users

Best for: iPhone users, Apple Watch users, walking, driving, and travelers who want useful offline routing inside the Apple ecosystem.

Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Apple Watch.

My verdict: Apple Maps is now a serious choice for iPhone travelers, especially if its coverage is strong in the destination you are visiting.

Apple Maps can support different types of travel, including:

  • Walking

  • Driving

  • Public transport

  • Cycling

  • Place searches

  • Offline maps

  • Turn-by-turn directions

Its offline features make it particularly interesting for international travel.

Apple Maps Has Strong Offline Navigation Features

On supported Apple devices and in supported areas, downloaded maps can provide more than simply a static map.

Offline features can include:

  • Driving directions

  • Walking directions

  • Cycling directions

  • Public transport routes

  • Estimated arrival information

  • Place details

This can be useful for travelers who expect periods without mobile data.

It is also one of the biggest practical differences between Apple Maps and Google Maps offline use.

Offline Walking Can Be a Major Advantage

Walking is one of the travel modes where Google Maps' offline limitations become more noticeable.

Apple Maps can provide offline walking directions in supported downloaded areas.

For an iPhone traveler spending several days sightseeing on foot, this can be valuable.

For example, you may still be able to navigate:

hotel → museum

even when mobile data has temporarily stopped working.

Apple Maps Features Vary by Destination

Do not assume every feature available at home will work exactly the same abroad.

Before departure, search some actual routes.

I would test:

  • Airport to hotel

  • Hotel to main station

  • Hotel to a major attraction

  • One public transport route

This gives you a better picture of whether Apple Maps is strong enough for the destination.

Apple Maps Works Well Across Apple Devices

If you already use several Apple devices, the experience can be convenient.

For example:

  • Look up a location on Mac

  • Continue navigation on iPhone

  • Receive directions on Apple Watch

You do not have to create a separate travel account or learn an unfamiliar app.

For people already comfortable with Apple Maps, that simplicity has value.

Apple Watch Can Be Useful While Walking

Apple Watch integration can reduce how often you need to hold your phone while walking through a city.

Directions can be easier to follow from your wrist when:

  • Walking between attractions

  • Leaving a station

  • Looking for a restaurant

This is a convenience feature.

I would still choose the app based on destination coverage first.

Apple Maps Public Transport

In supported destinations, Apple Maps can provide public transport information.

That can include different forms of local transit depending on the city.

As with every global navigation app, coverage and live information vary.

For important long-distance trains or airport services, confirm current information through the transport operator.

Apple Maps Is Not Automatically Better Because You Use an iPhone

If Google Maps gives much better destination information where you are traveling, use Google Maps.

The goal is not to stay loyal to an operating system.

The goal is to reach your destination easily.

Test both before departure if you are unsure.

Apple Maps Pros

  • Strong choice for iPhone users

  • Offline walking directions where supported

  • Offline driving

  • Offline cycling

  • Offline public transport in supported areas

  • Apple Watch integration

  • Simple Apple ecosystem experience

  • Turn-by-turn navigation

Apple Maps Limitations

  • Best suited to Apple devices

  • No Android app

  • Feature availability varies by country and city

  • Live information still depends on local data

  • Some destinations may have weaker place or transport coverage than others

Who Should Choose Apple Maps?

I would seriously consider Apple Maps if:

  • You use an iPhone.

  • It has strong coverage at your destination.

  • Offline walking matters.

  • You use Apple Watch.

  • You prefer the Apple ecosystem.

If you use Android, it is not an option.

If you use iPhone, I would test both Apple Maps and Google Maps on the routes that matter most before choosing your main app.

3. HERE WeGo — Best for Travelers Who Prioritize Offline Navigation

Best for: International travelers who expect weak mobile coverage, road trips, rental-car travel, and anyone who wants a navigation app built with offline use in mind.

Platforms: Android and iPhone.

My verdict: HERE WeGo is one of the first alternatives I would consider when dependable offline navigation matters more than restaurant reviews or a huge database of tourist places.

HERE WeGo supports:

  • Driving directions

  • Walking routes

  • Public transport information where available

  • Turn-by-turn guidance

  • Downloadable offline maps

  • Parking information

  • Traffic information while connected

Its main advantage is that offline navigation is treated as a central part of the app rather than simply an emergency backup. HERE currently describes WeGo as supporting walking directions and offline navigation alongside driving and other route options.

Why HERE WeGo Can Be Useful Abroad

Think about a trip where you plan to:

  • Rent a car

  • Drive outside major cities

  • Visit rural areas

  • Cross areas with weak reception

  • Limit roaming data

In those situations, a map that has been downloaded before departure can become much more useful than an app that depends heavily on a live connection.

HERE's current support guidance explains that offline navigation should be deliberately enabled in the app. If navigation begins in online mode and the connection disappears, HERE recommends restarting the route in offline mode rather than assuming the app will automatically behave exactly the same way.

That is a useful detail to know before losing reception.

Download Maps Before the Travel Day

If HERE WeGo is your offline backup, download the regions you need while you have reliable Wi-Fi.

Think beyond the hotel area.

Include:

  • Arrival airport

  • Main city

  • Rural destinations

  • Day trips

  • Driving route

Offline maps take storage space, so check your phone before downloading several countries.

Offline Navigation Is HERE WeGo's Main Strength

HERE's mapping technology supports downloaded regional data for offline:

  • Search

  • Routing

  • Mapping

  • Navigation

The company describes offline maps as a way to preload map data so core navigation can continue without consuming mobile data.

For travelers, that can be valuable when the route goes well outside major urban areas.

Live Information Still Benefits From Internet

Offline maps cannot reproduce every live feature.

For example, current traffic conditions depend on current information.

So I would think of HERE WeGo like this:

Offline: reliable base navigation

Online: navigation plus current traffic and other live information

If I were driving several hours through an unfamiliar country, I would still connect when possible so the route can respond to current road conditions.

HERE WeGo Can Be Useful for Public Transport Too

HERE WeGo also includes public transport routing and departure information in locations where the required data is available. Its current support materials include bus and train departure tools alongside other routing modes.

I would not choose HERE WeGo primarily for public transport if I were spending a week moving around a major transit-heavy city.

For that type of trip, a city-focused app may give you more useful detail.

HERE WeGo Pros

  • Strong offline focus

  • Downloadable maps

  • Turn-by-turn guidance

  • Good option for driving trips

  • Useful for areas with weak mobile service

  • Walking support

  • Public transport information in supported locations

  • Works on Android and iPhone

HERE WeGo Limitations

  • Some useful live information requires internet.

  • You need to understand how offline mode works before depending on it.

  • Business and tourist-place discovery may feel less central than in Google Maps.

  • Public transport depth varies by location.

Who Should Choose HERE WeGo?

I would consider HERE WeGo if:

  • Offline navigation is one of your biggest concerns.

  • You will rent a car.

  • You expect rural or low-signal travel.

  • You want a second map beside Google Maps or Apple Maps.

  • You prefer downloading larger areas before travel.

For a road trip, this is one of the stronger backup options on my list.

4. Citymapper — Best for Public Transport in Supported Cities

Best for: Travelers using metro, buses, trains, trams, ferries, and other public transport in major cities where Citymapper has strong coverage.

Platforms: Android and iPhone.

My verdict: Citymapper is not the app I would use as my only worldwide map, but it can become one of the most useful apps on your phone in a city it covers well.

Citymapper is built around urban transportation.

Depending on the city, it can combine routes involving:

  • Metro

  • Bus

  • Train

  • Tram

  • Ferry

  • Walking

  • Cycling

  • Other local transport options

Its main strength is helping you compare ways to move through a city rather than simply drawing a road from Point A to Point B.

Citymapper currently describes its service as covering major transit cities around the world.

Citymapper Makes More Sense in Dense Cities

Suppose you are staying in London.

You want to get from your hotel to an attraction.

There may be several realistic choices:

Tube

Bus

Train + walk

Walk the whole route

Citymapper is useful because public transport is the center of the experience.

That can make it particularly attractive in cities where visitors rarely need a rental car.

Check the City Before You Install It

This is important.

Do not assume Citymapper provides the same experience everywhere.

Before departure, check whether your destination is supported and test a real route.

Try:

Airport → hotel

and:

Hotel → main attraction

If the route information looks complete, the app may be very useful for the trip.

If your destination is not well covered, keep another app as your main navigation tool.

Citymapper Can Show More Than One Route Type

One useful feature of a transit-focused app is seeing different transport combinations.

You may find that:

Metro: 24 minutes

Bus: 29 minutes

Walk: 31 minutes

The fastest option is not always the option you want.

You might choose the bus because:

  • You have luggage

  • You want fewer stairs

  • You prefer seeing the city

  • The walking difference is small

This is where a public-transport app can help you make a practical decision rather than simply giving one route.

Citymapper Has Some Offline Support

Citymapper has offered offline access to certain information such as stored metro maps and scheduled departures.

Its own offline-support information explains that some metro and departure information can remain accessible after you lose your internet connection.

I would not treat that as complete offline route planning.

Before relying on it underground or without mobile data, test what remains available on your own phone.

Use Citymapper With Basic Local Transit Knowledge

Even a good app becomes easier to use once you understand:

  • How fares work

  • Whether tickets need validation

  • Which side of the platform you need

  • When the final service runs

If local transit systems are new to you, How to Use Public Transportation Abroad: ↗ A Beginner’s Guide explains the practical side of tickets, routes, transfers, and daily use.

That way Citymapper can handle the route while you still understand the system you are using.

Verify Important Service Changes

For an ordinary sightseeing journey, Citymapper can be extremely convenient.

For a connection you absolutely cannot miss, such as:

  • Airport train

  • Long-distance railway

  • Final service of the night

check the transport operator too.

Navigation apps depend on available transit data.

The operator remains the stronger source for a major cancellation or official schedule change.

Citymapper Pros

  • Strong urban public transport focus

  • Useful multimodal routes

  • Good for metro-heavy cities

  • Helps compare different ways to reach a place

  • Android and iPhone support

  • Some offline transport information

Citymapper Limitations

  • Coverage depends heavily on the city.

  • Not intended as a complete worldwide offline map.

  • Less useful for rural travel.

  • Not my first choice for long-distance driving.

  • Some live information naturally depends on current data.

Who Should Choose Citymapper?

I would install Citymapper when:

  • The destination is strongly supported.

  • Public transport will be my main way of getting around.

  • I am visiting a large city.

  • I want more transit detail than my general map provides.

I would normally use it alongside, rather than instead of, a general map.

5. Organic Maps — Best Free Privacy-Focused Offline Navigation App

Best for: Offline walking, hiking, cycling, driving, travelers limiting mobile data, and people who prefer an open-source map without advertising or tracking.

Platforms: Android and iPhone.

My verdict: Organic Maps is one of the most interesting backup navigation apps for international travelers because offline use is the normal experience rather than a secondary feature.

Organic Maps is built using OpenStreetMap data.

The project currently describes the app as:

  • Free

  • Open source

  • Privacy focused

  • Offline

  • Without ads

  • Without tracking

It supports travelers, hikers, cyclists, and drivers.

Organic Maps Is Built Around Offline Use

This is the biggest reason it appears on my list.

The app is intended to work with maps downloaded directly to the device.

Its current project information says:

  • Maps work offline

  • Search works offline

  • Routing works offline

  • Navigation works offline

rather than requiring a permanent internet connection.

That can make it a useful fallback when your normal map loses connection.

Organic Maps Can Be Particularly Useful for Walking and Hiking

Organic Maps has a strong focus on:

  • Walking

  • Hiking trails

  • Cycling

  • Outdoor routes

because its OpenStreetMap-based data can include smaller paths and trails contributed by the mapping community.

That makes it worth considering for trips involving:

  • Hiking

  • National parks

  • Rural walking

  • Cycling

  • Small streets

It does not mean every trail is automatically accurate or safe.

Always follow local signs, closures, and current conditions.

Privacy Is a Real Part of the Product

Organic Maps markets itself around minimal tracking rather than building a large advertising ecosystem around location searches.

For travelers who specifically care about that, it creates a clear reason to choose the app beyond simple navigation.

That does not automatically make it the best map for every trip.

It simply solves a different set of priorities.

The Trade-Off Is Live Information

The same app that works very well offline may not be the one I choose when my priority is:

  • Live traffic

  • Current public transport

  • Real-time road incidents

  • Large amounts of business information

Organic Maps is strongest when you want a dependable map stored on the phone.

It is less focused on the constantly changing live layer around that map.

Download Maps Before You Need Them

Organic Maps requires map data to be stored on your device.

Make sure you have:

  • Enough storage

  • Reliable Wi-Fi

  • The correct regions downloaded

The project's support information notes that map downloads can fail if storage is low or the network blocks the download.

Test the map before departure.

Organic Maps Pros

  • Free

  • No advertising

  • Strong privacy position

  • Open source

  • Works offline

  • Offline search

  • Offline routing

  • Walking and hiking focus

  • Cycling support

  • Driving navigation

  • Android and iPhone

Organic Maps Limitations

  • Less focused on live traffic.

  • Public transport information is not its main strength.

  • Business information can differ from large commercial mapping databases.

  • Crowd-sourced map information can vary between locations.

Who Should Choose Organic Maps?

I would consider Organic Maps if:

  • I want a reliable offline backup.

  • I will be hiking or walking extensively.

  • I want to reduce mobile-data dependence.

  • Privacy matters to me.

  • I do not need live traffic to be the main feature.

Even if Google Maps remained my primary app, Organic Maps could make sense as a lightweight offline backup for the right trip.

6. Waze — Best for Live Driving Information

Best for: Travelers renting cars, road trips with a working mobile-data connection, and drivers who care about live traffic and road reports.

Platforms: Android and iPhone.

My verdict: Waze is a driving specialist. I would choose it because I want current road information, not because I want one app for walking around a city.

Waze is built around live information contributed by its driving network.

It can provide:

  • Turn-by-turn driving navigation

  • Traffic information

  • Road hazards

  • Incidents

  • Route changes

  • Other driver-reported road conditions

Waze's current app information emphasizes real-time GPS navigation, traffic updates, and safety alerts.

Waze Makes the Most Sense While Driving

Imagine you are driving toward a city and there is suddenly:

  • Heavy traffic

  • An accident

  • A road closure

  • Another delay

Waze can use live road information to adjust the route.

That is very different from what I want from a walking map or metro app.

For a driving-heavy trip, that specialization can be valuable.

Waze Depends on Internet Connectivity

This is the major limitation for international travel.

Google's official Waze help states that Waze is built with the expectation of an active data connection. Without internet access, it cannot reliably locate and calculate a route, and live traffic and hazard information cannot function normally.

So I would not choose Waze as my only navigation option for a remote road trip.

I would keep an offline map available too.

Waze Is Not the Best Choice for Travelers Without Mobile Data

If you are planning to:

  • Drive through remote regions

  • Keep roaming disabled

  • Use a very small data plan

  • Spend long periods without signal

HERE WeGo or another offline-focused map may be a better base option.

You can still use Waze when connected.

Just do not build the entire trip around live features if you know connectivity may disappear.

Waze Is Not a Walking or Public Transport App

This is another reason I would not rank it as the overall best navigation app.

Waze is for driving.

If the trip is mainly:

walk → metro → museum → bus → hotel

you need a different tool.

But if the trip is:

rental car → highway → national park → another city

Waze becomes much more relevant.

Navigation Does Not Replace Driving Preparation

A map can tell you where to turn.

It cannot tell you whether you are legally ready to drive in another country.

Before renting a car, check licenses, insurance, local rules, road signs, and any permit requirements. International Driving Guide: ↗ Licenses, Rules, Insurance, and Safety covers those checks in more detail.

The app should guide your route, not become your entire driving preparation.

Waze Pros

  • Strong live traffic information

  • Driver-reported hazards

  • Turn-by-turn navigation

  • Useful rerouting

  • Good for driving-heavy trips

  • Android and iPhone support

Waze Limitations

  • Requires internet for normal route calculation and live features.

  • Not a full offline navigation solution.

  • Not built for walking.

  • Not built for public transport.

  • Less useful when mobile coverage is poor.

Who Should Choose Waze?

I would choose Waze if:

  • Driving is a major part of the trip.

  • I expect working mobile data.

  • Live traffic matters.

  • I want current road reports.

I would pair it with an offline navigation option for longer or more remote drives.

Which Navigation App Is Best for Each Type of Traveler?

After looking at the six apps, the choice becomes much easier when you start with the trip.

For a First International City Trip

Start with Google Maps.

It handles the widest mix of ordinary travel needs and works on both major phone platforms.

For an iPhone User Who Wants Offline Walking

Consider Apple Maps.

Its offline routing can be particularly useful in supported destinations.

For a Rental-Car Trip With Weak Mobile Coverage

Consider HERE WeGo.

Its offline navigation focus fits this type of trip well.

For a Public-Transport-Heavy City Trip

Check Citymapper.

If the destination has strong Citymapper coverage, it can be an excellent transit companion.

For Hiking or Offline Walking

Consider Organic Maps.

Its offline-first approach and OpenStreetMap foundation make it useful for this type of travel.

For Driving With Live Traffic

Use Waze.

But keep another option available if the trip passes through areas with poor reception.

Can You Use Two Navigation Apps on the Same Trip?

Yes.

This is one area where having two apps can make sense because they may solve different problems.

For example:

Google Maps + Citymapper

General locations + detailed city transit

Google Maps + Organic Maps

Everyday navigation + offline backup

Waze + HERE WeGo

Live driving + offline driving backup

Apple Maps + Google Maps

Apple offline features + Google's broad place database

You do not need to keep every route open in both apps.

Use the second app when it provides something the first one does not.

Do Not Switch Apps in the Middle of Driving

If one navigation app gives a route you did not expect, do not start comparing three phones while moving.

Pull over somewhere safe when necessary.

Then:

  • Check the route

  • Compare another app

  • Look at the road situation

  • Continue once you understand the change

A navigation app should reduce distraction, not create more of it.

A Navigation App Can Still Be Wrong

No map is perfect.

Problems can include:

  • Closed roads

  • Incorrect entrances

  • New construction

  • Private roads

  • Old business locations

  • Missing paths

Pay attention to:

  • Road signs

  • Barriers

  • Local authorities

  • Transport staff

  • Your surroundings

If the app tells you to enter somewhere that is clearly closed or restricted, do not follow it simply because the blue line says so.

The map is a tool.

The real environment comes first.

Free Navigation Apps Are Enough for Most Travelers

Most travelers do not need to pay for a navigation app.

Google Maps, Apple Maps, HERE WeGo, Organic Maps, Citymapper, and Waze all provide useful core functions without requiring every traveler to buy a premium navigation subscription.

Before paying for anything, ask:

  • What feature am I missing?

  • Will I use it repeatedly?

  • Does the free version already solve my main problem?

  • Is there another free app that handles that one task better?

For an ordinary international vacation, I would normally spend money on mobile connectivity before paying simply to access another map app.

Do Not Choose a Navigation App Only Because It Is Free

Free is useful, but reliability matters more.

Imagine having:

  • A free map

  • A rental car

  • Four hours of rural driving

  • No mobile signal

If that app cannot handle the route offline, saving a few dollars has not helped much.

Match the app to the situation first.

Then compare cost.

Navigation Apps Can Use a Lot of Battery

Travel navigation can keep several phone features active at the same time:

  • GPS

  • Screen

  • Mobile data

  • Location services

  • Live traffic

  • Public transport updates

Add photos, translation, messaging, and ticket apps, and your phone may use battery much faster than it does at home.

I would not leave the hotel at 20% battery if I expected to depend on my phone for directions all day.

Start with a charged phone.

A small power bank can also be useful on long sightseeing or driving days.

Reduce Battery Use Without Losing Your Map

If your battery begins running low:

  • Lower screen brightness.

  • Lock the screen between route checks where practical.

  • Use voice instructions while driving.

  • Close unnecessary high-use apps.

  • Keep the phone out of extreme heat.

  • Use downloaded maps where they meet your needs.

Do not disable important location functions while actively depending on navigation just to save a small amount of power.

Save battery in places that do not remove the tool you still need.

Navigation Apps Can Also Use Mobile Data

Normal map navigation may not consume as much data as video streaming, but live features still require a connection.

Data use can increase through:

  • Map loading

  • Traffic updates

  • Public transport information

  • Business searches

  • Photos

  • Reviews

If your travel plan has a small data allowance, downloading map areas before departure can reduce some of that dependence.

Large software updates, video uploads, and entertainment usually deserve more attention than ordinary route guidance when trying to conserve data.

Download Maps on Wi-Fi

Before leaving home or your accommodation:

  • Connect to reliable Wi-Fi.

  • Download the areas you need.

  • Check available phone storage.

  • Open the downloaded map.

  • Test what works without a connection.

Do not simply press Download and assume the job is finished.

Keep Enough Free Phone Storage

Offline maps can take up significant space, especially if you download:

  • Several countries

  • Large states or provinces

  • Multiple city regions

Before a long trip, check whether your phone also needs room for:

  • Photos

  • Videos

  • Translation languages

  • Music

  • Travel documents

You may not need the entire country when you are visiting one city.

Download based on the actual route.

Delete Old Offline Maps After the Trip

A map you downloaded for last year's trip may still be using phone storage.

After returning home, review:

  • Offline maps

  • Local transport apps

  • Old downloaded tickets

  • Temporary travel files

Remove what you no longer need.

This keeps space available for your next trip.

Protect Your Location Privacy

Navigation apps naturally need location access to provide live directions.

That does not mean every app on your phone needs permanent access to your location.

Review your phone's location permissions.

Depending on your preferences and the app, you may choose access such as:

  • While using the app

  • Approximate location

  • Precise location only where needed

A navigation app generally needs accurate location while you are actively navigating.

A completely unrelated app probably does not need to know where you are all day.

Be Careful With Public Location Sharing

Some map apps allow you to share your live location with:

  • Family

  • Friends

  • Travel companions

This can be useful.

For example, two people exploring separately may share locations before meeting again.

But share that information intentionally.

Avoid publicly broadcasting your exact live movements when there is no reason to do so.

Save Your Accommodation Before You Arrive

This is one of the simplest navigation habits I use.

Before departure, save:

  • Hotel

  • Hostel

  • Apartment

  • Resort

Then you do not need to type a long unfamiliar address while carrying luggage after a flight.

I would also keep the full accommodation address available separately from the map.

If your phone loses service, you can still show the address to:

  • Taxi driver

  • Transport staff

  • Hotel staff

  • Someone helping you locally

Save the Location, Not Only the Hotel Name

Hotels can have:

  • Similar names

  • Several branches

  • Multiple properties in one city

Always check the exact address before saving it.

This is particularly important with chain hotels.

Getting directions to the correct brand but the wrong branch can send you several miles away from your booking.

Check Airport Routes Before Flying

Before departure, search:

Airport → accommodation

Look at:

  • Distance

  • Public transport

  • Driving time

  • Walking after the final station

  • Late-night options

You do not have to memorize the entire route.

You should know roughly what happens after landing.

This makes it easier to notice if a driver or transport option does not match what you expected.

Check Which Airport You Are Actually Using

Large destinations can have several airports.

For example, searching only:

London airport

or:

Paris airport

is not enough.

Check the airport code on your booking.

A route planned from the wrong airport can change:

  • Travel time

  • Cost

  • Train options

  • Hotel arrival time

The map can only help if you give it the correct starting point.

Train Stations Can Have Similar Names Too

The same problem appears with stations.

A city can have:

  • Main railway station

  • Regional station

  • Airport station

  • Metro station with a similar name

Compare the station name in the navigation app with the station printed on your ticket.

Do not choose the first search result because it looks familiar.

Search for the Correct Entrance

Some stations, shopping centers, airports, stadiums, and attractions have several entrances.

Your map may bring you to the building while your actual entrance is on another street.

Before an important timed visit, look for:

  • Gate number

  • Entrance name

  • Terminal

  • Meeting point

This is especially useful when the property covers a large area.

Add Extra Time Around Large Stations

A route saying:

12 minutes by train

does not mean the entire journey takes 12 minutes.

You may also need time to:

  • Find the entrance

  • Buy or validate a ticket

  • Walk through the station

  • Change platforms

  • Find the exit

Navigation apps estimate routes.

They cannot perfectly predict how long a first-time traveler needs to find their way through an unfamiliar transport hub.

Do Not Follow the Estimated Arrival Time Too Literally

A navigation app might say:

Arrival: 9:47 AM

Treat that as an estimate.

Your real arrival can change because of:

  • Traffic

  • Missed turns

  • Crowds

  • Road closures

  • Slow walking

  • Transport delays

If you have:

  • Flight

  • Train

  • Timed attraction

  • Tour

build in extra time.

Do not plan an important journey so tightly that one missed pedestrian crossing ruins the schedule.

Check the Last Public Transport Service

A navigation app may show you how to reach a restaurant at 8:00 PM.

You also need to know how you will return at 11:30 PM.

If you plan to stay out late, check:

  • Final metro

  • Final bus

  • Night service

  • Taxi or ride option

before leaving.

This is particularly useful when staying outside the city center.

Do Not Assume Tomorrow's Route Will Be Identical to Today's

Transport and roads can change because of:

  • Weekend schedules

  • Construction

  • Events

  • Demonstrations

  • Weather

  • Service maintenance

Check important journeys again on the day you travel.

A route you saved several weeks earlier is useful for planning.

It should not automatically become your final live route.

For Major Transport, Use the Operator as the Final Check

Navigation apps are excellent for finding routes.

But if you are catching:

  • Intercity train

  • Airport express

  • Ferry

  • Long-distance bus

check the actual operator for important last-minute information.

That is where you are more likely to find authoritative details about:

  • Cancellation

  • Departure change

  • Ticket restriction

  • Platform information

Use your map to reach the service.

Use the operator to confirm the service itself.

Do Not Treat Map Reviews as Official Information

Google Maps and other apps can contain:

  • Reviews

  • Photos

  • User answers

These can help you understand what a place is like.

They are not always reliable enough for important facts.

For example, if a review says:

You can enter without a reservation

but the attraction's current official rules require timed tickets, the official rule matters.

Use map information as practical context.

Verify important booking requirements separately.

Do Not Let Navigation Replace Basic Awareness

It is easy to stare at the blue route line and stop looking around.

Keep enough awareness to notice:

  • Traffic

  • Road signs

  • Station signs

  • Closures

  • People around you

  • The environment

If the app says:

Turn left

but left is clearly a closed construction zone, do not walk into it because the map has not updated.

Use Headphones Carefully While Walking

Voice directions can be convenient.

But avoid turning the volume so high that you cannot hear:

  • Vehicles

  • Bicycle bells

  • Train announcements

  • People around you

This is especially important when the destination's road system is unfamiliar.

You can use audio navigation without removing all awareness of the street.

A Navigation App Should Not Replace Common Sense at Night

The shortest walking route may not always be the route you want late at night.

An app may suggest:

  • Empty side street

  • Park

  • Narrow alley

  • Poorly lit route

because it saves a few minutes.

You are allowed to choose:

  • Busier road

  • Main street

  • Taxi

  • Another route

A map calculates distance and time.

It does not perfectly measure how comfortable every street will feel to you at a particular hour.

Use Navigation to Support Your Itinerary

A navigation app is also useful before the trip for checking whether your daily plans make geographic sense.

For example, if four attractions are spread across opposite sides of a city, the map will show it immediately.

Your main itinerary can stay inside your chosen planning system. If you still need one, Best Travel Planning Apps for Organizing Your Trip ↗ compares the main trip-organizing options.

Then use your navigation app for the actual routes.

Common Navigation App Mistakes Travelers Make

Downloading the App but Not the Map

Installing an app does not automatically make the destination available offline.

Download what you need.

Assuming Offline Means Every Feature Works

Test walking, driving, and other route types separately.

Downloading Too Small an Area

Include the airport, accommodation, and major day-trip routes.

Never Testing the Map Before Departure

Use airplane mode at home and see what happens.

Depending on Only One Digital Map

Keep at least the most important addresses somewhere else.

Choosing an App Without Checking Destination Coverage

The highest-rated app may not be the best app in your specific city.

Using Citymapper Where It Has Weak Coverage

Check the destination first.

Using Waze as an Offline Backup

Waze depends heavily on an active connection.

Assuming Google Maps Has Full Offline Walking

It does not provide the normal full walking-routing experience when offline.

Ignoring Apple Maps Because of Its Older Reputation

Current Apple Maps is much more capable than early versions, especially for Apple users in supported regions.

Following a Route Into an Obviously Restricted Area

Real road signs, closures, and local instructions come first.

Depending Only on Live Traffic

Traffic can change after your route begins.

Leave reasonable time for important journeys.

Searching Only by Business Name

Confirm the exact branch and address.

Forgetting Which Airport or Station Is on Your Ticket

Use the booking details, not your memory.

Waiting Until Your Battery Is Nearly Dead

Navigation becomes much harder when your phone switches off.

Looking at the Phone While Driving

Set the route before moving and use safe hands-free guidance where permitted.

Planning a Zero-Buffer Airport Journey

Navigation time is an estimate, not a guarantee.

  • You use Android.

  • You travel internationally.

  • You mix walking, driving, and public transport.

  • Finding businesses and attractions matters.

  • You usually have mobile data.

My pick: Best overall.

Choose Apple Maps If...

  • You use an iPhone.

  • Your destination has strong coverage.

  • Offline walking matters.

  • You use Apple Watch.

  • You prefer Apple's map experience.

My pick: Best for Apple users.

Choose HERE WeGo If...

  • Offline routing is a high priority.

  • You will drive.

  • You expect poor mobile coverage.

  • You want a strong offline backup.

My pick: Best offline-focused option.

Choose Citymapper If...

  • You are visiting a supported major city.

  • You expect to use public transport heavily.

  • Comparing metro, bus, train, and walking options matters.

My pick: Best city transit specialist.

  • You want free offline maps.

  • Privacy matters.

  • You will walk, hike, or cycle.

  • You do not need live traffic to be the main feature.

My pick: Best privacy-focused offline app.

  • You will drive regularly.

  • You have working mobile data.

  • Live traffic and road incidents matter.

My pick: Best live driving specialist.

Frequently Asked Questions

For most travelers, I would start with Google Maps because of its broad international coverage and mix of walking, driving, public transport, and place information.

Google Maps has broader cross-platform use and strong place information. Apple Maps can be particularly useful for iPhone travelers who want supported offline walking and transit routing.

HERE WeGo and Organic Maps are strong offline-focused options. Apple Maps also provides useful offline navigation on supported Apple devices and destinations.

Its normal offline mode does not provide full walking directions. Downloaded maps remain useful, but offline functions differ from the connected experience.

Citymapper can be better for detailed public transport in a well-supported city, while Google Maps is a stronger general-purpose option across more travel situations.

Final Thoughts

In short, The best navigation app is the one that works for your destination, phone, transport style, and internet situation. Google Maps is the easiest starting point for most international travelers, while Apple Maps, HERE WeGo, Citymapper, Organic Maps, and Waze each become stronger in particular situations. Choose your main app before departure, save the locations that matter, download offline coverage, test it without internet, and keep enough backup information that one phone or connection problem does not leave you unable to find your way.
