Best VPNs for Travelers: When You Need One and What to Look For

✓ Practical guidance⌖ International travel✦ Updated August 2026

A VPN can be useful while traveling, but I would not tell every traveler that they must pay for one before leaving home. Modern websites and apps already use encryption for much of the traffic we send online, and the FTC notes that public Wi-Fi is generally safer than it once was because encrypted connections are now widespread. A VPN still adds another privacy layer, especially when you regularly use hotel, airport, café, or other shared networks.

When I compare VPNs for travel, I care less about flashy claims such as “military-grade protection” and more about things I can actually check: independent audits, logging policy, kill switch, modern VPN protocols, device limits, server locations, app support, ease of use, and whether the company clearly explains how its service works.

For this guide, I checked current provider information and recent audit records available in August 2026. My goal is to help you decide whether you need a VPN at all, what it can realistically protect, and which services make the most sense for different types of travelers.

!

VPN Note: VPN features, prices, server locations, device limits, and local laws can change. Check the current plan and the rules at your destination before subscribing or relying on a VPN abroad.

What Is a VPN?

VPN stands for Virtual Private Network.

When you connect to a VPN service, your device creates an encrypted connection to a VPN server.

Your internet traffic first travels through that connection.

Instead of a website seeing your normal public IP address, it generally sees the IP address of the VPN server.

For example:

Without VPN:

Phone → hotel Wi-Fi → internet → website

With VPN:

Phone → encrypted VPN tunnel → VPN server → internet → website

This changes who can easily see certain parts of the connection.

It does not make everything happening online invisible.

What Does a VPN Actually Hide?

A VPN can help prevent the local network from easily seeing the destinations and traffic moving through the VPN tunnel.

That can matter when you connect through:

  • Hotel Wi-Fi

  • Airport Wi-Fi

  • Café Wi-Fi

  • Hostel Wi-Fi

  • Shared accommodation networks

Your internet provider also sees that you are connected to a VPN rather than seeing your normal direct internet destinations in the same way.

Websites you visit generally see the VPN server's IP address rather than your normal public IP.

A VPN Moves Trust Rather Than Removing It

This is one of the most important things to understand before buying one.

Without a VPN, you trust your:

  • Internet provider

  • Mobile carrier

  • Local Wi-Fi network

With a commercial VPN, you are also placing significant trust in the VPN company.

That company operates the server your traffic passes through.

This is why I give much more weight to:

  • Clear privacy policies

  • Independent audits

  • Transparent infrastructure

  • Established security practices

than to advertisements promising complete anonymity.

Does a VPN Make You Anonymous?

No.

A VPN can hide your normal public IP address from websites, but there are many other ways your activity can still be connected to you.

For example, if you sign into:

  • Google

  • Facebook

  • Instagram

  • Amazon

  • Your airline account

the service still knows which account you are using.

Websites may also use:

  • Cookies

  • Browser information

  • Account details

  • Device identifiers

A VPN improves network privacy.

It does not erase your digital identity.

A VPN Does Not Change Your GPS Location

This is another common misunderstanding.

A VPN changes the internet-facing IP address used by your connection.

It does not normally change the GPS location reported by your phone.

An app with permission to access your precise location may still know where the device physically is.

So:

VPN server in London

does not automatically mean:

phone GPS says London.

These are different location systems.

A VPN Does Not Make a Scam Website Safe

Suppose you connect through a VPN and then visit a fake banking website.

The VPN may encrypt the connection between your device and its server.

It cannot stop you from voluntarily entering your password into the scam website.

The FTC specifically warns that even a fraudulent website can use encryption and appear to have a secure HTTPS connection. Encryption protects the connection; it does not prove that the person operating the website is trustworthy.

A VPN cannot replace careful browsing.

A VPN Does Not Replace Two-Factor Authentication

You should still protect important accounts with:

  • Strong unique passwords

  • Two-factor authentication

  • Updated software

  • Device screen lock

These protect different risks.

For example, a VPN does little to help if someone already knows your email password.

Do You Need a VPN for Public Wi-Fi?

This answer deserves more nuance than:

“Never use airport Wi-Fi without a VPN.”

Most modern websites use HTTPS.

The FTC says the widespread use of encryption means public Wi-Fi is generally safe for ordinary browsing, and encrypted websites protect information moving between your browser and the website.

That does not mean every public network should automatically be trusted.

Fake access points, poorly configured networks, certificate warnings, and unencrypted connections can still create risks.

A reputable VPN gives you an additional encrypted layer between the device and VPN server.

When I Would Use a VPN on Public Wi-Fi

I would be more likely to connect a VPN when using:

  • Airport Wi-Fi

  • Hotel Wi-Fi

  • Hostel Wi-Fi

  • Café Wi-Fi

  • Conference Wi-Fi

  • Another network shared with many unknown users

especially if I expect to spend a long time online.

A current Swiss cybersecurity advisory for travelers recommends using only networks you recognize, paying attention to security warnings, and considering a trusted VPN when you have doubts about a public network.

Mobile Data Can Be a Simpler Alternative

If my phone has reliable mobile data, I often prefer it to an unknown public Wi-Fi network.

That avoids joining the local shared Wi-Fi network at all.

It does not make mobile internet completely private.

Your mobile carrier is still part of the connection.

But from a practical travel perspective, using your own mobile connection can remove some of the uncertainty around an unknown hotel or café network.

A VPN can still be used over mobile data if you want the additional privacy layer.

Do You Need a VPN for Hotel Wi-Fi?

Not always.

A well-run hotel network combined with modern HTTPS may be fine for normal everyday browsing.

I would still consider a VPN if:

  • I use hotel Wi-Fi frequently.

  • I am working with sensitive business information.

  • I do not know how the network is managed.

  • I want to reduce what the local network can observe.

The same reasoning applies to hostels and shared accommodation.

What About Airport Wi-Fi?

Airport Wi-Fi is convenient because travelers often need to:

  • Check transport

  • Message family

  • Open bookings

  • Work during a layover

But airports also have large numbers of users and can be places where fake networks appear.

Always check the official Wi-Fi name.

Do not automatically connect to something simply because it is called:

Airport Free WiFi

A VPN can add protection after you connect, but it cannot tell you that every network name is legitimate.

A VPN Can Be Useful for Remote Work

A VPN becomes more important when the trip involves work.

Your employer may already require a company VPN to connect to internal systems.

That is different from buying a consumer VPN such as NordVPN or Proton VPN.

If your company has a required security setup, follow its rules.

Do not replace a work VPN with a personal consumer VPN because they may serve different purposes.

Do You Need a VPN for Online Banking Abroad?

A VPN is not automatically required for banking.

Modern banking websites and apps already use encrypted connections.

In some situations, suddenly appearing to log in from a VPN server or another country could even trigger additional fraud checks.

For banking, I care more about:

  • Using the official app or website

  • Strong account security

  • Two-factor authentication

  • Avoiding suspicious links

  • Keeping the device updated

If you choose to use a VPN, use a reputable service.

Do not install a random free VPN just before opening a financial account.

A VPN Can Change Your Visible IP Location

VPN services usually allow you to connect through servers in different locations.

For example, while physically in Spain, you might connect to a server in:

  • Spain

  • France

  • United Kingdom

  • United States

Websites may then treat your IP address as coming from that server location.

This can sometimes affect:

  • Search results

  • Website language

  • Regional versions of services

  • Content availability

However, services can detect or block VPN addresses.

I would never buy a VPN based on a promise that every website or streaming platform will always work from every country.

That can change at any time.

Check Local Rules Before Using a VPN

VPN rules are not identical worldwide.

Some countries place restrictions on:

  • VPN services

  • Particular providers

  • Internet access

  • Certain websites

Rules can also change.

Before traveling somewhere with heavy internet restrictions, check the current local law and official travel guidance.

Do this before departure, not after discovering that your VPN app or website is unavailable.

What Should You Look for in a Travel VPN?

This is where I become much more selective.

A VPN handles sensitive network traffic.

I would not choose one simply because:

“It has five stars in the app store.”

Here are the factors I consider most important.

1. A Clear No-Logs Policy

A good VPN should clearly explain what information it:

  • Collects

  • Does not collect

  • Stores

  • Deletes

The phrase no logs alone is not enough.

I prefer services that have allowed independent specialists to review whether their systems match their claims.

2. Independent Audits

An audit does not guarantee that a VPN will never have a security problem.

But it is stronger evidence than the provider simply saying:

“Trust us.”

I look for companies that repeatedly allow external firms to examine:

  • Server systems

  • Apps

  • Privacy controls

  • No-logs claims

Recent audits matter more than one review performed many years ago.

3. Kill Switch

A kill switch can stop internet traffic from leaving normally if the VPN connection unexpectedly fails.

For example:

VPN connected → network changes → VPN disconnects

Without a kill switch, some traffic could continue through the normal connection.

With a properly working kill switch, the app can block that traffic until protection returns.

This is especially useful when moving between:

  • Hotel Wi-Fi

  • Mobile data

  • Airport Wi-Fi

4. Modern VPN Protocols

The protocol controls how the VPN connection is built.

For a normal traveler, I would not spend hours manually tuning protocols.

I mainly want the provider to support modern, well-established options such as:

  • WireGuard

  • OpenVPN

  • A well-audited provider-specific protocol

WireGuard has become particularly common because it can combine strong security with good performance.

5. Apps for the Devices You Carry

Before subscribing, check every device you expect to use.

For example:

  • Android phone

  • iPhone

  • Windows laptop

  • MacBook

  • Tablet

Do not assume one subscription supports the same features on every operating system.

6. Enough Simultaneous Connections

A solo traveler may have:

phone + laptop

A couple may have:

2 phones + 2 laptops + tablet

A family can quickly have many more.

Check how many devices can connect at the same time, not only how many apps can be installed.

7. Server Locations That Match Your Trips

More servers are not automatically better.

I care about whether the provider has useful locations:

  • Near my destination

  • Near my home region

  • In countries I regularly use

A nearby server can often give better performance than routing traffic halfway around the world.

8. Easy Connection on Hotel and Airport Networks

Travel networks can behave differently from your home Wi-Fi.

Some require a browser login page before the internet works.

A VPN should be simple enough that you understand how to:

  • Join the Wi-Fi.

  • Complete the network login.

  • Connect the VPN.

Complicated setup is not helpful when you are standing in an airport.

9. Reliable Support

Travel problems rarely happen at convenient times.

If the VPN stops connecting abroad, useful support can matter.

I give some value to:

  • Good help documentation

  • Clear troubleshooting

  • Responsive support

especially for beginner travelers.

10. Sensible Pricing

VPN pricing can be confusing.

Providers often advertise a low:

“per month”

price that actually requires paying for:

  • One year

  • Two years

  • Longer promotional period

Renewal pricing may also be different from the first payment.

I compare the total amount charged and renewal conditions rather than the large discounted number on the front page.

Does a VPN Use Extra Mobile Data?

Yes, a VPN adds some overhead because your traffic has to be encrypted and wrapped inside the VPN connection.

For most normal travel use, that does not mean your data allowance suddenly doubles.

But if you have a very small mobile-data plan, remember that VPN use is not completely free from a data perspective.

Heavy activities such as:

  • Video streaming

  • Cloud backup

  • Large downloads

will still consume much more data than ordinary browsing.

A VPN Can Reduce Speed

Routing traffic through another server adds another step to the connection.

That can increase:

  • Latency

  • Connection time

and sometimes reduce speed.

The effect depends on:

  • VPN provider

  • Server load

  • Distance to the server

  • Local internet quality

  • VPN protocol

If the connection feels slow, the first thing I would try is a nearby server.

Do Not Connect Halfway Around the World Without a Reason

Choose a faraway location only when you have a reason to need that location.

VPN Speed Is Limited by the Internet Underneath It

A VPN cannot turn bad hotel Wi-Fi into fast internet.

If the hotel connection itself provides:

2 Mbps

a VPN cannot magically create:

200 Mbps.

The VPN can protect the connection.

It does not create bandwidth that does not exist.

What If a Hotel or Airport Network Blocks the VPN?

This can happen.

If it still fails:

  • Try another VPN server.

  • Let the app choose an automatic protocol.

  • Try a provider-supported alternative protocol or obfuscation option.

  • Check the VPN's official troubleshooting instructions.

Do not change advanced settings randomly.

One setting at a time makes troubleshooting much easier.

What Does a Kill Switch Look Like in Real Travel?

Imagine you are working on hotel Wi-Fi.

Your VPN is connected.

Then the hotel network briefly drops and reconnects.

A working kill switch can block that traffic during the interruption.

For travelers who keep the VPN active for privacy, this is one of the features I consider genuinely useful rather than just marketing.

Common VPN Mistakes Travelers Should Avoid

Assuming a VPN Makes You Anonymous

It hides your normal public IP from websites, but accounts, cookies, device information, and information you provide can still identify you.

Choosing a VPN Only by Server Count

Having thousands of servers sounds impressive.

Useful locations, reliability, security, and privacy evidence matter more.

Installing an Unknown Free VPN

A VPN receives access to sensitive network traffic.

Research the provider first.

Believing Every “No-Logs” Claim

Look for independent audits and clear policies where possible.

Ignoring Renewal Pricing

A cheap first term can become a much more expensive renewal.

Buying Several Years for One Short Trip

Make sure you will actually use the subscription afterward.

Connecting to a Distant Server Without a Reason

This may make your connection slower.

Leaving the VPN Off on the Networks You Bought It For

If public Wi-Fi privacy was your reason for subscribing, remember to actually connect.

Assuming the VPN Secures a Fake Website

A phishing site can still steal information you voluntarily enter.

Ignoring HTTPS Warnings

Do not continue through serious browser certificate warnings simply because the VPN is connected.

Using a Personal VPN Instead of a Required Work VPN

Follow your employer's security policy.

Expecting a VPN to Improve Bad Wi-Fi

It cannot create bandwidth that the underlying connection does not have.

Assuming VPN Use Is Treated the Same Everywhere

Check current rules for your destination.

Waiting Until Arrival to Install It

Set up and test the service before departure.

In short, I would not treat a VPN as mandatory travel technology, but I would consider one if I regularly use shared networks, work while traveling, or want an extra privacy layer between my devices and the networks I connect to. NordVPN offers the strongest overall balance for most travelers, Proton VPN is my preferred privacy and free starting option, Mullvad stands out for simple privacy-focused pricing, ExpressVPN suits travelers who value ease of use, and Surfshark makes the most sense for families with many devices.
