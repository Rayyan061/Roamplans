Open-Jaw Flights Explained: What They Are and When to Choose Them (Beginner's Guide)

A practical beginner's guide to flying into one city, traveling onward independently, and returning home from another without unnecessary backtracking.

✦ Multi-city route planning ✓ Less backtracking ↕ Compare time and total cost

I first discovered open-jaw flights while planning a trip through ↗ Italy. My original plan was to fly into Rome and return home from Rome. After looking at my itinerary, I realized I would spend the entire trip traveling north until I reached Milan. Going all the way back to Rome just to catch my return flight didn't make much sense. Instead, I booked an open-jaw ticket, flew into Rome, explored the country at my own pace, and returned home from Milan. It saved both time and unnecessary travel.

If you've never heard of an open-jaw flight, you're not alone. Many travelers aren't familiar with the term until they begin planning a multi-city trip. This guide explains what an open-jaw flight is, how it works, when it's useful, and how it compares with other flight options. By understanding this ticket type, you can build more efficient travel itineraries and avoid unnecessary backtracking.

What Is an Open-Jaw Flight?

An open-jaw flight is an airline itinerary where you arrive in one city and depart from another instead of returning to the same airport.

Unlike a traditional round-trip ticket, your arrival and departure airports are different.

For example:

  • ✓
    Fly from New York to Rome.
  • ✓
    Travel through Italy by train.
  • ✓
    Fly home from Milan to New York.

Instead of returning to Rome for your flight home, you continue your journey across the country and depart from a different city.

Why Is It Called an Open-Jaw Flight?

The name comes from the shape of the itinerary.

If you draw the flights on a map, the route doesn't form a complete loop.

Instead, there's an "open" section between the city where you arrive and the city where you depart.

For example:

  • ✓
    Flight into City A.
  • ✓
    Travel by train, car, or another method to City B.
  • ✓
    Flight home from City B.

That missing section between the two cities creates the "open jaw."

How Does an Open-Jaw Flight Work?

An open-jaw itinerary combines two flights into one journey.

A simple example looks like this:

  • ✓
    Depart from Los Angeles.
  • ✓
    Arrive in Paris.
  • ✓
    Travel independently through France.
  • ✓
    Depart from Nice.
  • ✓
    Return to Los Angeles.

Your transportation between Paris and Nice isn't included in the airline ticket.

Instead, you arrange that part of the journey yourself using trains, buses, rental cars, or domestic flights.

How Is an Open-Jaw Flight Different from a Round-Trip Ticket?

Although both options include an outbound and return flight, they work differently.

Round-Trip Flight

  • ✓
    Arrive and depart from the same city.
  • ✓
    Simple itinerary.
  • ✓
    Best for returning to your starting point.

Open-Jaw Flight

  • ✓
    Arrive in one city.
  • ✓
    Depart from another.
  • ✓
    Allows continuous travel without returning to your original arrival airport.

Open-jaw tickets often make more sense for travelers exploring several destinations in one region.

How Is It Different from a One-Way Ticket?

A one-way ticket only covers one part of your journey.

An open-jaw ticket still includes both your outbound and return flights.

The difference is simply that your return flight begins from a different city.

For example:

One-Way Flight

  • ✓
    London → Tokyo

Open-Jaw Flight

  • ✓
    London → Tokyo
  • ✓
    Osaka → London

Instead of booking two completely separate one-way tickets, many travelers choose an open-jaw itinerary for this type of journey.

Instead of returning to the city where your trip began, you continue moving forward through your destination.

This often means:

  • ✓

  • ✓
    Greater travel flexibility.

For longer trips, this can create a much smoother travel experience.

This allows you to move naturally through the country without repeating the same journey in reverse.

Think About Your Itinerary First

One of the biggest advantages of an open-jaw ticket is that it supports the way many people actually travel.

Before booking, ask yourself:

  • ✓
    Will I visit several cities?
  • ✓
    Do I plan to travel in one direction?
  • ✓
    Would returning to my arrival city waste time?
  • ✓
    Can I easily travel between destinations?

If the answer to these questions is yes, an open-jaw flight may be a much better option than a traditional round-trip ticket.

When Does an Open-Jaw Flight Make Sense?

An open-jaw flight is most useful when you don't plan to return to the city where your trip begins.

Instead of traveling back to your arrival airport, you simply continue your journey and fly home from your final destination.

An open-jaw ticket is a great choice if you:

  • ✓
    Plan to visit several cities.
  • ✓
    Want to travel in one direction.
  • ✓
    Don't want to waste time backtracking.
  • ✓
    Have a road trip planned.
  • ✓
    Intend to use trains or buses between destinations.

For these types of trips, an open-jaw itinerary often feels more natural than a traditional round-trip ticket.

Are Open-Jaw Flights More Expensive?

Many travelers assume that open-jaw tickets always cost more.

In reality, pricing depends on several factors, including:

  • ✓
    Airline.
  • ✓
    Route.
  • ✓
    Travel season.
  • ✓
    Flight availability.
  • ✓
    Demand.

Sometimes an open-jaw ticket costs about the same as a traditional round-trip ticket.

Other times, it may cost slightly more or even less.

Instead of making assumptions, compare both options before booking.

Instead of spending an extra day returning to your original arrival city, you can use that time exploring another destination or simply enjoying a more relaxed travel schedule.

For travelers planning to move continuously through one region, an open-jaw itinerary often creates a much more efficient and enjoyable journey.

Common Open-Jaw Flight Mistakes

Open-jaw flights can make a trip much more efficient, but they require a little more planning than a traditional round-trip ticket.

Over the years, I've noticed that most problems happen because travelers focus only on the flights and forget about the journey between the two cities.

Some of the most common mistakes include:

  • ✓
    Booking an open-jaw ticket without planning transportation between cities.
  • ✓
    Underestimating travel time between destinations.
  • ✓
    Assuming all airports are close to the city center.
  • ✓
    Comparing only ticket prices instead of the total trip cost.
  • ✓
    Forgetting to check baggage rules if using different airlines.
  • ✓
    Booking too many destinations into a short trip.
  • ✓
    Not researching local transportation options.
  • ✓
    Choosing an itinerary that doesn't match your travel plans.

A little preparation before booking can help you enjoy the flexibility of an open-jaw itinerary without unnecessary stress.
