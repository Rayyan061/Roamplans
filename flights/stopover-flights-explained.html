Stopover Flights Explained: What They Are and When to Choose Them (Beginner's Guide)

A practical beginner's guide to understanding stopovers, comparing them with layovers, planning extra city time, checking entry rules, and deciding when a longer connection improves your trip.

✦ Stopover vs. layover ✓ Extra destination planning ↕ Costs, visas, and timing

The first time I booked a stopover flight, I thought it would simply make my journey longer ↗. Instead, it gave me the chance to spend almost a full day exploring another city before continuing to my final destination. What I expected to be an inconvenience became one of the most memorable parts of the trip. Since then, I've learned that a stopover can sometimes add more value to a journey than taking the fastest possible flight.

If you've ever seen the word "stopover" while booking a flight and weren't sure what it meant, you're not alone. Many travelers confuse stopovers with layovers or assume they're exactly the same. This guide explains what a stopover flight is, how it works, its advantages and disadvantages, and when choosing one can make your trip more enjoyable.

What Is a Stopover Flight?

A stopover flight is a journey where you intentionally spend an extended period in another city before continuing to your final destination.

Unlike a short connection at an airport, a stopover usually gives you enough time to leave the airport and explore the city if you wish.

For example:

  • ✓
    Fly from New York to Paris.
  • ✓
    Spend two days in Paris.
  • ✓
    Continue from Paris to Rome.

Although your final destination is Rome, Paris becomes an additional stop on your journey.

Stopover vs. Layover

Many travelers use these two terms interchangeably, but they don't mean exactly the same thing.

Layover

A layover is a short stop between flights.

It usually lasts a few hours and is mainly intended for changing planes before continuing your journey.

Stopover

A stopover is a much longer planned stop.

It often gives travelers enough time to leave the airport, visit the city, or even stay overnight before boarding the next flight.

The main difference is the amount of time you spend at the connecting destination.

Why Do Airlines Offer Stopovers?

Some airlines allow stopovers because they operate through major hub airports.

Instead of simply changing planes, passengers may have the option to spend additional time in the connecting city before continuing their trip.

Airlines may offer stopovers to:

  • ✓
    Promote tourism.
  • ✓
    Give travelers more flexible itineraries.
  • ✓
    Encourage passengers to explore another destination.
  • ✓
    Increase the appeal of certain flight routes.

For travelers, this can sometimes mean visiting two cities during one vacation instead of only one.

How Does a Stopover Work?

Although every airline has its own booking process, the general idea is simple.

Your trip is divided into two parts.

For example:

  • ✓
    Fly from your departure city to the stopover destination.
  • ✓
    Spend several hours or several days there.
  • ✓
    Continue to your final destination.

Everything can often be arranged under one itinerary, depending on the airline and the ticket you choose.

Why Some Travelers Choose Stopover Flights?

Not everyone books the fastest possible route.

Some travelers intentionally choose stopovers because they enjoy seeing an extra destination along the way.

A stopover can provide:

  • ✓
    An opportunity to explore another city.
  • ✓
    Time to rest before a long flight.
  • ✓
    A chance to experience another culture.
  • ✓
    More flexible travel plans.
  • ✓
    A more memorable journey.

For many people, the journey itself becomes part of the vacation instead of simply a way to reach the destination.

The Benefits of a Stopover

A well-planned stopover can offer several advantages.

Some of the biggest benefits include:

  • ✓
    Visiting two destinations during one trip.
  • ✓
    Breaking up a long journey.
  • ✓
    Reducing travel fatigue.
  • ✓
    Exploring a new city.
  • ✓
    Enjoying local food and attractions.
  • ✓
    Making long-haul travel feel more comfortable.

For travelers who enjoy sightseeing, a stopover can add extra value without requiring a completely separate vacation.

Stopovers Can Make Long Flights Feel Easier

Very long flights can be physically tiring.

After spending many hours sitting on an airplane, having the opportunity to leave the airport, stretch your legs, and rest in another city can make the remainder of the journey much more comfortable.

Instead of spending twenty or more hours traveling continuously, some travelers prefer dividing the journey into smaller, more manageable parts.

This approach may not be right for every trip, but it can make long-distance travel feel less exhausting.

Think About Your Travel Goals

Before choosing a stopover flight, ask yourself why you're traveling.

If your goal is to arrive as quickly as possible, a direct or shorter route may be the better choice.

However, if you enjoy exploring new places and have extra time available, a stopover may allow you to experience an additional destination without planning a completely separate trip.

Choosing the right flight depends on your priorities rather than following one travel rule.

When Is a Stopover Flight a Good Choice?

A stopover flight isn't the right option for every traveler.

However, it can be a great choice in certain situations.

You might consider booking a stopover if you:

  • ✓
    Have a flexible travel schedule.
  • ✓
    Want to visit another city during your trip.
  • ✓
    Don't mind extending your journey.
  • ✓
    Enjoy exploring new destinations.
  • ✓
    Want to break up a long-haul flight.

For travelers who see the journey as part of the adventure, a stopover can add a completely new experience to the trip.

When Should You Avoid a Stopover?

Although stopovers have many advantages, they aren't always the best option.

A stopover may not be suitable if you:

  • ✓
    Have limited vacation time.
  • ✓
    Need to reach your destination quickly.
  • ✓
    Are traveling for a short business trip.
  • ✓
    Have young children who may struggle with longer travel.
  • ✓
    Prefer the simplest possible journey.

In these situations, choosing a direct flight or a shorter connection may be the better option.

Do Stopover Flights Cost More?

Many travelers assume that adding a stopover will automatically increase the ticket price.

In reality, the cost depends on several factors, including:

  • ✓
    The airline.
  • ✓
    Your destination.
  • ✓
    Travel season.
  • ✓
    Ticket availability.
  • ✓
    Length of the stopover.

Sometimes a stopover flight costs about the same as a standard connecting flight.

Other times, spending extra time in the stopover city may increase your overall travel costs because of accommodation, meals, and local transportation.

Instead of assuming one option is cheaper, compare the complete trip cost before booking.

Think About Accommodation Costs

If your stopover includes an overnight stay, you'll probably need accommodation.

Before booking, consider:

  • ✓
    Hotel prices.
  • ✓
    Transportation between the airport and city.
  • ✓
    Check-in and check-out times.
  • ✓
    Distance from the airport.

Adding these expenses to your travel budget helps you understand the true cost of choosing a stopover.

Check Visa and Entry Requirements

One detail that's easy to overlook is whether you're allowed to leave the airport during your stopover.

Depending on your nationality and the country where your stopover takes place, you may need:

  • ✓
    A visa.
  • ✓
    Transit permission.
  • ✓
    Additional travel documents.

Always check the latest entry requirements before booking your flight.

Taking a few minutes to research these rules can help you avoid unexpected problems after arriving.

Plan How You'll Spend Your Stopover

If you have enough time to leave the airport, planning your visit in advance helps you make the most of it.

Depending on the length of your stopover, you might:

  • ✓
    Visit famous landmarks.
  • ✓
    Try local food.
  • ✓
    Walk around the city center.
  • ✓
    Visit a museum.
  • ✓
    Relax in a nearby park.
  • ✓
    Stay overnight before your next flight.

Even a well-planned half-day stop can become one of the highlights of your trip.

Example of a Stopover Flight

Imagine you're traveling from Los Angeles to Tokyo.

Instead of taking the fastest available route, you choose an itinerary that allows you to spend two days in Seoul before continuing to Japan.

Your journey could look like this:

  • ✓
    Fly from Los Angeles to Seoul.
  • ✓
    Explore Seoul for two days.
  • ✓
    Fly from Seoul to Tokyo.

Instead of visiting only one destination, you enjoy two cities during the same vacation.

This is one reason why many travelers enjoy stopover flights.

Leave Enough Time Before Your Next Flight

One advantage of a stopover is that you aren't rushing to catch your next flight within a couple of hours.

Even so, it's important to manage your time carefully.

Before heading back to the airport, allow enough time for:

  • ✓
    Transportation to the airport.
  • ✓
    Security screening.
  • ✓
    Immigration if required.
  • ✓
    Airline check-in.
  • ✓
    Boarding.

Treat your second flight just like the beginning of a new journey instead of assuming everything will happen automatically.

Stopovers Work Best with Flexible Travel Plans

From my own experience, stopovers are most enjoyable when I'm not trying to fit too much into a tight schedule.

If I only have a few vacation days, I'd rather spend them at my main destination.

But when I have extra time, adding another city to the journey often makes the trip even more memorable.

The key is choosing a stopover because it improves your travel experience—not simply because it's available.

Common Stopover Flight Mistakes

A stopover can be a great travel experience, but only if it's planned carefully.

Over the years, I've found that most stopover problems happen because travelers underestimate the amount of planning involved.

Some of the most common mistakes include:

  • ✓
    Not checking visa or entry requirements.
  • ✓
    Booking a stopover that's too short to enjoy.
  • ✓
    Booking a stopover that's longer than your schedule allows.
  • ✓
    Forgetting to budget for hotels and meals.
  • ✓
    Not researching transportation from the airport.
  • ✓
    Assuming all airlines offer the same stopover options.
  • ✓
    Trying to see too much in a limited amount of time.
  • ✓
    Forgetting to leave enough time to return to the airport.

A little planning before your trip can help you enjoy the stopover instead of feeling rushed.

Stopover Flight Checklist

Before booking a stopover flight, use this simple checklist.

Before Booking

  • ✓
    Confirm your destination.
  • ✓
    Decide how long you want to stay.
  • ✓
    Compare total trip costs.
  • ✓
    Check airline stopover options.
  • ✓
    Review visa or entry requirements.

Planning Your Stopover

  • ✓
    Book accommodation if staying overnight.
  • ✓
    Plan transportation from the airport.
  • ✓
    Research local attractions.
  • ✓
    Check opening hours if visiting popular sites.
  • ✓
    Keep copies of important travel documents.

Before Your Next Flight

  • ✓
    Confirm your departure terminal.
  • ✓
    Leave enough time to reach the airport.
  • ✓
    Complete check-in if required.
  • ✓
    Keep your passport and boarding pass ready.
  • ✓
    Double-check your luggage arrangements.

Following these steps can help make your stopover smooth and enjoyable.
