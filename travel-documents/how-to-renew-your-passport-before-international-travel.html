How to Renew Your Passport Before International Travel?

✦ Renewal timing ✓ Online & mail options ↕ Urgent travel checks

Renewing a passport sounds simple ↗ until an international trip is already booked and the expiration date is getting close. The real question is often not whether the passport has expired, but whether it will still meet the destination's entry rules by the time you travel.

Renew your passport before international travel when it will not satisfy the destination's required validity period, lacks required blank pages, is damaged, or no longer matches your legal identity. Start before booking non-refundable travel whenever possible, allowing for the published processing time, delivery in both directions, possible corrections, visa updates, and unexpected delays.

For U.S. travelers, the correct path may be online renewal, mail renewal, or an in-person application when renewal eligibility does not apply. Confirm the current method, fee, processing estimate, and urgent-travel procedure with the U.S. Department of State before submitting. First review the destination-specific passport requirements for international travel.

When Should You Renew Your Passport Before International Travel?

Do not choose your renewal date based only on the expiration date printed inside the passport.

Start by checking three things:

  1. 1
    When does your passport expire?
  2. 2
    When are you traveling?
  3. 3
    How much remaining validity does your destination require?

For example, your passport could remain valid for four months after you return home but still fail the entry rules of a country requiring more remaining validity.

That means a passport does not need to be expired for renewal to make sense.

The supporting RoamPlans guide Passport Requirements for International Travel explains passport validity, blank-page rules, damage, transit requirements, and other entry checks in more detail.

A Simple Way to Decide

Consider renewing before travel if:

  • ✓
    Your passport expires soon after the trip
  • ✓
    Your destination requires more remaining validity
  • ✓
    You expect to travel again soon
  • ✓
    Your passport has very few usable pages
  • ✓
    Your passport details need to change
  • ✓
    Your current passport may not qualify for the trip you are planning

Do this check before making non-refundable bookings where possible.

How Early Should You Renew Your Passport?

There is no benefit in planning around the exact processing estimate if you have plenty of time.

Apply earlier.

As of August 2026, the U.S. Department of State lists:

Service Current Processing Time
Routine 4–6 weeks
Expedited 2–3 weeks
Urgent travel Appointment may be available for qualifying travel within 14 calendar days

These processing estimates do not include mailing time. The State Department says an application may take up to two weeks to reach them and another two weeks for the completed passport to reach you after it is mailed.

So a routine application listed as 4–6 weeks should not be treated as a promise that the passport will be in your hand exactly four to six weeks after you mail the application.

Give Yourself Extra Time

I would avoid building a trip around the shortest possible processing estimate.

Extra time helps if:

  • ✓
    Your application has an error
  • ✓
    Your photo is rejected
  • ✓
    Additional information is requested
  • ✓
    Mail takes longer than expected
  • ✓
    Passport demand increases
  • ✓
    Your travel date changes
  • ✓
    You later discover that you also need a visa

The State Department says processing times can change and demand is generally highest between late winter and summer.

Should You Renew Before Booking Your Flight?

If your passport is close to expiration, check it before booking.

You do not always need your new passport in hand before buying a flight, but you should know whether renewal could affect the trip.

Start by checking:

  • ✓
    Passport expiration
  • ✓
    Destination validity requirement
  • ✓
    Travel date
  • ✓
    Visa requirements
  • ✓
    Transit-country requirements
  • ✓
    Current passport processing times

Then look at your booking conditions.

Flexible or refundable travel can reduce risk while waiting for a new document. A highly restricted ticket gives you fewer options if the passport does not arrive when expected.

Be Careful with Visas

Passport renewal can matter even more when the trip requires a visa.

Visas and electronic authorizations are often linked to a particular passport number.

If you apply using your current passport and then replace it before travel, you may need to update the visa information or apply again, depending on the destination.

Check that country's official rules before deciding whether to renew before or after applying for the visa.

Can You Renew a U.S. Passport Online?

Yes, some U.S. adults can currently renew their passports online.

The official U.S. online renewal service is run by the Department of State. The government warns travelers against unofficial websites that claim they can submit an online passport renewal on your behalf.

Who Can Renew Online?

Current Department of State rules say you must meet all of its online eligibility conditions.

Among them:

  • ✓
    Your most recent passport was valid for 10 years.
  • ✓
    It expires within one year or expired less than five years ago.
  • ✓
    You are age 25 or older.
  • ✓
    You are not changing personal information such as your name or sex.
  • ✓
    You are not traveling for at least six weeks after submitting the application.
  • ✓
    You are physically in a U.S. state or territory when you submit it.
  • ✓
    The passport is in your possession.
  • ✓
    It is not damaged.
  • ✓
    It has not been reported lost or stolen.

Online renewal currently uses routine service only. It cannot be expedited.

When Online Renewal Makes Sense

Online renewal can work well when:

  • ✓
    Your trip is still months away
  • ✓
    Your passport meets all eligibility rules
  • ✓
    You do not need expedited processing
  • ✓
    Your personal information has not changed
  • ✓
    You have your current passport

It is less suitable if your departure date is close.

Your Old Passport Becomes Unusable

Once you submit an eligible online renewal, the Department of State says your most recent passport will be canceled and should no longer be used for international travel.

Keep that in mind if you have another international trip planned while the renewal is being processed.

Can You Renew a Passport by Mail?

Many eligible adults can renew a U.S. passport by mail using Form DS-82.

Current Department of State rules allow mail renewal when your most recent passport:

  • ✓
    Can be submitted with the application
  • ✓
    Has never been reported lost or stolen
  • ✓
    Was issued within the last 15 years
  • ✓
    Was issued when you were age 16 or older
  • ✓
    Was valid for 10 years
  • ✓
    Is not damaged beyond normal wear
  • ✓
    Uses your current name, or you can provide accepted evidence of a legal name change

If you do not meet the requirements, you may need to apply in person instead.

Why Some Travelers Choose Mail Renewal?

Mail renewal gives eligible travelers an option that online renewal does not currently provide:

expedited service.

The State Department currently lists expedited passport processing at 2–3 weeks for an additional fee, excluding mailing time.

This can make mail renewal more suitable when your trip is too close for online renewal but not yet inside the urgent-travel window.

When You Cannot Renew Your Passport Normally

Some travelers need to apply for a passport again instead of using the standard adult renewal process.

The Department of State says an adult generally needs an in-person application when the previous passport was:

  • ✓
    Lost
  • ✓
    Stolen
  • ✓
    Damaged
  • ✓
    Issued more than 15 years ago
  • ✓
    Issued when the traveler was under age 16

These cases normally use Form DS-11 rather than the standard mail-renewal process.

Children Under 16 Do Not Renew Passports

This point causes confusion for families.

A U.S. passport issued to a child under age 16 is generally valid for five years, but it c annot be renewed. The child must apply for a new passport.

So if you are planning a family trip, check every passport separately.

Do not put a child's expired passport into the adult renewal process.

What If Your International Trip Is Less Than 14 Days Away?

If international travel is very close, normal processing may no longer fit your schedule.

The Department of State currently allows qualifying travelers with international travel within 14 calendar days to seek an appointment at a passport agency or center. Travelers who need a foreign visa may qualify based on travel within the next 28 days.

You will need proof of qualifying international travel.

Appointments are limited, and the Department of State says it does not charge a fee simply to schedule one. It also warns against third parties selling passport-agency appointments.

If You Already Submitted a Passport Application

Do not submit another application just because your travel date is approaching.

The Department of State instructs travelers who already applied and now have urgent plans to contact Passport Services. They may try to move the existing application forward or arrange an agency appointment if needed.

Keep your application locator number available because it helps identify where your application is being processed.

What Do You Need to Renew a U.S. Passport?

The exact documents depend on whether you renew by mail or online.

For a normal adult renewal by mail, you will usually need:

  • ✓
    Completed Form DS-82
  • ✓
    Your most recent U.S. passport
  • ✓
    One new passport photo
  • ✓
    Legal name-change document if your name has changed
  • ✓
    Correct passport renewal fee

The State Department requires mail-renewal applicants to submit their most recent passport with Form DS-82. If your legal name is different, you must also include a certified copy of an accepted name-change document.

Online renewal is different because you keep your old passport rather than mailing it. You need your current passport details, a digital passport photo, payment information, Social Security number, and other personal information requested in the online application.

How to Complete Form DS-82 for Passport Renewal

Form DS-82 is the standard passport renewal form for eligible adults renewing by mail.

Before using it, make sure you actually qualify for mail renewal.

The State Department currently allows DS-82 mail renewal when your most recent passport meets its conditions, including being issued within the last 15 years, issued when you were at least 16, originally valid for 10 years, available to submit, and not badly damaged or reported lost or stolen.

Complete Every Required Field

The State Department offers an online Form Filler that can help reduce typing and form errors. After completing the form, print it on single-sided paper. Double-sided applications are not accepted for this process.

Check information such as:

  • ✓
    Full legal name
  • ✓
    Date of birth
  • ✓
    Place of birth
  • ✓
    Social Security number where requested
  • ✓
    Current address
  • ✓
    Contact information
  • ✓
    Previous passport details
  • ✓
    Emergency contact

Compare passport numbers and dates directly with your passport rather than entering them from memory.

Sign and Date the Form

Do not forget the signature.

The State Department tells mail-renewal applicants to sign and date Form DS-82 before sending it.

A missing signature or date is also one of the reasons Passport Services may contact an applicant and put the application on hold.

Do You Send Your Old Passport When Renewing?

It depends on how you renew.

Renewing by Mail

Yes.

You must send your most recent passport with the application. The State Department returns the old passport separately, and it may arrive after the new passport.

This means you should not plan another international trip using that passport while the application is being processed.

Renewing Online

Do not mail your old passport when using the official online renewal process.

You keep it, but the Department of State cancels it after you submit the online renewal. It can no longer be used for international travel after cancellation.

This is one reason I would check all upcoming trips before starting a renewal.

Passport Photo Requirements for Renewal

Passport photos cause more application problems than many travelers expect.

The State Department says unacceptable photos are the number one reason passport applications are placed on hold.

For a paper renewal, you need one color passport photo taken within the last six months.

Printed Passport Photo Size

The printed photo must be:

2 x 2 inches, or 51 x 51 mm.

Your head must also fall within the required size range in the image.

Basic Passport Photo Rules

Your photo should:

  • ✓
    Be in color
  • ✓
    Be recent
  • ✓
    Show you facing the camera
  • ✓
    Show your full face
  • ✓
    Have a neutral expression
  • ✓
    Have both eyes visible
  • ✓
    Use a plain white or off-white background
  • ✓
    Have clear, even lighting
  • ✓
    Be sharp rather than blurry or pixelated
  • ✓
    Be free from filters or edits that change your appearance

These requirements come directly from current State Department passport-photo guidance.

Do Not Edit the Background

A common mistake is taking a photo against the wrong background and trying to replace it using editing software.

The State Department requires the image to be original and warns against digital edits that change the background, face, head, or natural appearance.

Take the photo correctly from the start.

Passport Photo for Mail Renewal

For Form DS-82 mail renewal, follow the attachment instructions on the application.

Current State Department instructions tell applicants to staple the photo using four staples placed vertically near the outer corners and not to bend the photo.

Passport Photo for Online Renewal

Online applicants upload a digital passport photo instead of mailing a printed one.

Use the official digital-photo instructions rather than uploading a scanned copy of an old printed passport photo.

How Much Does It Cost to Renew a U.S. Passport?

As of August 2026, current State Department adult renewal fees are:

Passport Document Renewal Fee
Passport book $130
Passport card $30
Passport book and card $160

These fees apply to renewal-eligible adults using mail or online renewal.

Expedited Passport Fee

If you renew by mail and need faster processing, current expedited service costs an additional:

$60

The State Department currently lists expedited processing at about 2–3 weeks, excluding mailing time.

Online renewal currently does not offer expedited processing.

Faster Return Delivery

You can currently pay an additional $23.36 for 1-to-3-day delivery of an issued passport book.

This faster delivery option affects the return shipment after the passport is issued. It does not reduce the government's application-processing period.

Is There a $35 Acceptance Fee for Renewal?

Normally, no.

Eligible adults renewing with Form DS-82 pay the passport application fee but do not pay the $35 acceptance-facility fee used for many DS-11 applications. The State Department specifically says postal employees should not charge a $35 facility fee for a DS-82 mail renewal.

How Do You Pay for Passport Renewal?

Payment depends on your renewal method.

Renewing by Mail

For DS-82 mail renewal, the State Department currently instructs applicants to use a:

  • ✓
    Personal check, or
  • ✓
    Money order

The payment should be made payable to the U.S. Department of State. Do not send cash.

Write the applicant's full name and date of birth on the front of the check or money order as instructed.

Renewing Online

Online renewal accepts payment by credit or debit card through the official application system.

Be careful with websites that look official but charge extra money to submit an online renewal.

The State Department says its official online passport renewal service is the only authorized online submission system and warns that third-party sites may charge extra fees or put personal information at risk.

How to Renew a Passport by Mail Step by Step?

For an eligible adult, the mail process can be broken into a few clear steps.

Step 1: Confirm You Qualify

Check that your most recent passport meets the DS-82 renewal requirements.

If it was lost, stolen, badly damaged, issued too long ago, or issued when you were younger than 16, you may need an in-person application instead.

Step 2: Complete Form DS-82

Fill in all required information.

Print the form single-sided, then sign and date it.

Step 3: Include Your Current Passport

Put the passport you are renewing with your application.

If you have both a passport book and card and are renewing both, follow the State Department instructions for which documents to submit.

Step 4: Add Name-Change Documents if Needed

If the name on your renewal application differs from your most recent passport, include the required certified legal evidence.

Common examples are:

  • ✓
    Marriage certificate
  • ✓
    Divorce decree
  • ✓
    Court-ordered name change document

The State Department returns these documents separately.

Step 5: Add Your Passport Photo

Use a current photo that follows the official size, background, lighting, and appearance rules.

Step 6: Add the Correct Payment

Calculate:

Passport fee + expedi ted fee if selected + optional faster return delivery

Double-check the amount before mailing.

Wrong or missing fees are one reason Passport Services may stop processing and contact the applicant.

Step 7: Mail the Application

Mail DS-82 applications through the address listed for your location and service choice.

The State Department says the mailing address can differ based on where you live and whether you request routine or faster service.

Follow the address shown in the current official instructions rather than copying an old mailing address from a blog article.

How to Renew a Passport Online Step by Step?

If you meet all current eligibility rules, online renewal removes the need to mail Form DS-82 and your old passport.

Step 1: Confirm Online Eligibility

Online renewal is currently limited to eligible U.S. citizens age 25 or older whose passport meets specific age, validity, condition, location, personal-information, and travel-timing requirements.

If your trip is less than six weeks away, the online method is not currently available because online applications cannot be expedited.

Step 2: Gather Your Information

Have ready:

  • ✓
    Passport being renewed
  • ✓
    Digital passport photo
  • ✓
    Credit or debit card
  • ✓
    Social Security number
  • ✓
    Emergency contact
  • ✓
    Current contact information

The official online system asks for this information during the application.

Step 3: Complete Your Own Application

The State Department requires applicants to complete their own online renewal. A third-party company cannot officially submit it for you.

Step 4: Upload Your Digital Photo

Use a recent image that meets the separate online passport-photo rules.

Do not use filters or alter your natural appearance.

Step 5: Pay the Fee

Pay through the official online system.

The standard document fees are currently the same basic amounts used for adult renewal by mail.

Step 6: Submit Only When You Are Ready

Once the online renewal is submitted, the passport being renewed is canceled for international travel.

I would check my calendar one final time before clicking submit.

If another international trip suddenly appears during the processing period, the old passport cannot simply be used because it is still physically in your possession.

How to Renew a Passport After a Name Change?

A marriage, divorce, or legal name change can affect which process and form you use.

Name Changed and Passport Can Be Renewed by Mail

If you qualify for normal DS-82 mail renewal, the State Department requires a certified copy of the legal document showing the name change. Examples include a marriage certificate, divorce decree, or court order.

Recent Name Change After a Recently Issued Passport

If both the passport issuance and legal name change happened within the relevant one-year period, the State Department may allow use of Form DS-5504 instead. Current guidance says there is normally no standard fee for this type of qualifying update, although expedited service costs extra.

No Legal Name-Change Document

If you use a different name but cannot provide an accepted legal name-change document, the normal renewal route may not work.

Current State Department instructions may require an in-person DS-11 application with extra evidence showing use of the new name.

Check the exact situation before choosing a form.

Submitting the wrong passport form can delay your travel plans.

Common Passport Renewal Mistakes That Cause Delays

Review the broader travel document mistakes guide so the new passport details also match visas, tickets, and travel authorizations.

A renewal can take longer when the application is incomplete or does not meet the rules.

The State Department lists several common reasons it may contact an applicant for more information.

1. Forgetting to Sign or Date the Form

This is easy to miss after printing an application.

Check the signature before placing DS-82 in the envelope.

2. Using a Bad Passport Photo

A photo can be rejected because of:

  • ✓
    Wrong size
  • ✓
    Poor lighting
  • ✓
    Shadows
  • ✓
    Blurry image
  • ✓
    Wrong background
  • ✓
    Digital editing
  • ✓
    Old photo
  • ✓
    Incorrect head position

Passport-photo problems are a common cause of applications being put on hold.

3. Paying the Wrong Fee

Check the current fee based on whether you want:

  • ✓
    Passport book
  • ✓
    Passport card
  • ✓
    Both
  • ✓
    Expedited service
  • ✓
    Faster return delivery

Do not use an old fee amount saved from a previous application.

4. Forgetting the Old Passport

Mail applicants must include their most recent qualifying passport. Missing it can stop normal processing.

5. Using DS-82 When You Are Not Eligible

If your old passport does not meet renewal rules, you may need to apply in person instead.

Trying to force an ineligible passport through the renewal process wastes time.

6. Waiting Until the Trip Is Too Close

The easiest passport problem to avoid is poor timing.

Processing times do not include all mailing time, and an application issue can add more delay.

Start early enough that a simple correction does not threaten the trip.

7. Applying Through an Unofficial Website

Do not assume a website is official because its name contains words such as "passport," "government," or "Gov."

The Department of State advises users to make sure the online renewal site ends in . gov and warns against unauthorized companies collecting fees and personal data.

How to Track Your Passport Renewal Application?

After you submit a passport renewal, you can track its progress through the official U.S. passport status system.

If you included an email address with your application, the State Department can also send status updates by email. To check manually, you generally need your last name, date of birth, and the last four digits of your Social Security number.

Do not worry if your application does not appear immediately.

The State Department says it can take up to two weeks after applying before the application shows as "In Process." For a mailed application, delivery tracking may show that your package arrived before Passport Services has entered it into its own system.

What Do Passport Application Statuses Mean?

The status system uses a few common updates.

In Process

This means a passport agency or processing center is reviewing your application.

How long it remains in this stage depends on the service you selected and current processing times.

Approved

"Approved" means the application review is complete and Passport Services is preparing to print your passport.

Occasionally, an application may move back to "In Process" after showing as approved if an issue is found during the final review.

Passport Mailed

This means your new passport has been sent to the mailing address on your application.

Passport books are sent through a trackable delivery service. Passport cards are normally sent separately by First Class Mail.

Supporting Documents Mailed

Your old passport and other supporting documents may arrive separately from your new passport.

The State Department says these supporting documents can arrive up to four weeks after your new passport book or card.

What If Passport Services Asks for More Information?

Sometimes Passport Services needs more information before it can complete an application.

Your status may show Additional Information Needed.

This means your application is on hold while the State Department waits for you to respond. Current guidance says applicants generally have 90 days from the date of the letter or email to provide the requested information.

Possible issues can include:

  • ✓
    Missing signature
  • ✓
    Incorrect or missing payment
  • ✓
    Passport photo problems
  • ✓
    Missing previous passport
  • ✓
    Missing documents
  • ✓
    Information that needs clarification

Respond as soon as possible because time spent waiting for your reply can extend the overall processing period.

What If Your Passport Renewal Status Is Not Showing?

If your application was submitted recently, give the system time to update.

The State Department says it may take up to two weeks for an application to appear as "In Process."

If more than two weeks have passed, check what happened to your application.

For a mailed renewal:

  1. 1
    Check your mailing tracking number.
  2. 2
    Confirm whether the payment has been processed.
  3. 3
    Check the online passport status system.
  4. 4
    Make sure your name is entered exactly as it appeared on the application.

If Passport Services has processed your payment but you still cannot find the application status, current State Department guidance says you can contact the National Passport Information Center for help.

What If Your Passport Is Taking Longer Than Expected?

Start by comparing the application date with the full travel timeline, not just the government's processing estimate.

As of August 2026, the State Department lists routine processing at 4–6 weeks and expedited processing at 2–3 weeks. It also says mailing can take up to two weeks before processing begins and another two weeks after the passport is mailed back to you.

So the full timeline can be longer than the number shown under "processing time."

If Your Trip Is More Than 14 Days Away

If you have already applied and your trip is more than 14 days away, the State Department says you can contact Passport Services for certain changes, such as requesting expedited service or faster delivery of the completed passport.

If Your Trip Is Within 14 Days

If you have already applied and will travel internationally within 14 days, current State Department guidance says to contact the National Passport Information Center.

A passport-agency appointment may be available, but availability is not guaranteed.

Do not submit a second passport application unless Passport Services specifically tells you to do so.

Your New Passport Has a New Passport Number

A renewed passport does not keep the same passport number as the old one.

This matters because other travel records may still contain the previous number.

After receiving the new passport, check whether you need to update:

  • ✓
    Airline passenger details
  • ✓
    Visa records
  • ✓
    eVisa
  • ✓
    Electronic travel authorization
  • ✓

For U.S. Trusted Traveler programs such as Global Entry, NEXUS, and SENTRI, the State Department specifically tells travelers to update their account with the new passport number, issue date, and expiration date.

What Happens to a Visa in Your Old Passport?

Do not assume that renewing your passport automatically cancels or transfers a foreign visa.

The correct answer depends on the country that issued the visa.

The State Department tells travelers who still have an active visa in an old passport to check with the foreign embassy or consulate that issued that visa to see whether it can still be used.

A destination may:

  • ✓
    Allow the valid visa to remain in the old passport
  • ✓
    Require you to carry both passports
  • ✓
    Require the visa details to be updated
  • ✓
    Require a new visa

Check before departure.

Do not remove a physical visa from the old passport.

What About a eVisa or Electronic Travel Authorization?

Electronic visas and travel authorizations are often linked to the passport details entered during the application.

Since a renewed passport has a new passport number, an authorization linked to the old document may need to be updated or replaced.

The rule depends on the destination.

Before traveling, check the official immigration or embassy instructions for the country that issued the authorization.

This is especially worth checking if you completed the visa or authorization before renewing your passport.

Will You Get Your Old Passport Back?

Normally, yes, when it was submitted as part of an eligible passport renewal.

The State Department returns old passports and other supporting documents separately from the new passport. Those documents can arrive up to four weeks after your new passport.

Do not panic if the new passport arrives first.

If more than four weeks have passed since the new passport arrived and the supporting documents have still not been returned, the State Department advises contacting Passport Services.

Keep the old passport if it contains:

  • ✓
    Active foreign visas
  • ✓
    Useful travel records
  • ✓
    Previous entry stamps
  • ✓
    Personal travel history

But once it has been canceled for renewal, do not try to use it as your active passport.

Look at your destination requirements again.

Some countries require several months of remaining passport validity beyond the dates of travel. The State Department also warns that airlines may refuse boarding if a traveler does not meet the destination's passport-validity requirement.

Check:

Final Thoughts

The best time to deal with passport renewal is before it starts controlling the rest of your trip.

I prefer checking the passport as soon as international travel becomes likely. That gives enough room to compare the expiration date with destination rules, choose the correct renewal method, deal with processing time, and update visas or bookings after the new passport arrives.

For U.S. travelers, the renewal process now offers both online and mail options for eligible adults, but your travel date should guide which method makes sense. Current processing times can also change, so check the State Department again when you are ready to apply.

Once your new passport arrives, check its details immediately and update any travel records connected to the old passport number. That final step can prevent a small renewal issue from becoming an airport problem.
